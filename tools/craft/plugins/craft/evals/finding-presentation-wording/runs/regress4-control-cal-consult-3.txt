The panel's main finding is that the shared spreadsheet would let members double-book a lane or lose a booking without being told. Both serious problems come from that, and the small database avoids both.

**What the reviewers found.** The four reviewers mostly found one problem. With the spreadsheet, the sign-up list has more than one writer at a time: the form, two members going for the last place in a lane, and the volunteer's Friday tidy-up. Nothing decides which change wins. So a booking can be given out twice or quietly lost, and the member finds out when they arrive at the pool. Three smaller problems sit beside that:
- The sheet is shared by link, so anyone with the link can read every member's phone number.
- Members get no clear confirmation and have no way to cancel.
- Some members can't or won't use an online page at all.

Two things are real but small: copying the sign-ups out when the trial ends, and going back to paper partway through the term.

**Whether it holds.** The two serious findings hold. They follow directly from the spreadsheet set-up the question describes, not from guesses about scale. They aren't among the concerns that the trial's early stage is allowed to make less serious. A member turning up for a lane they don't hold means the trial has failed by its own measure. The phone-number finding is also correct, and it too only arises with a shared sheet. The ease-of-use reviewer's top concern was that members might find the page harder than paper. That went too far: it guesses at how members will feel and names no actual failure, so I moved it to the middle group. Its more specific point stands: members without a phone can't book. Two reviewers suggest keeping the paper sheet pinned up, and that is sensible. But it would become a third list for the same places and bring back double booking, unless someone enters the paper names through the form.

**What to do.** Put the sign-ups in the small database from the start. That makes the form the only thing that writes bookings. The form can then check how many places a lane has left before confirming, and phone numbers stay out of a link-shared sheet. That one choice settles the three most important findings. Replace the Friday tidy-up with a read-only list for the volunteer. If the committee still prefers the spreadsheet, it only works with three rules:
1. The form checks how many places a lane has left before confirming.
2. The tidy-up happens on a copy, after sign-ups close.
3. Phone numbers stay off the sheet.

Whichever you choose, add:
- a confirmation screen that names the lane and the time
- a cancel link
- a short note in the newsletter
- a paper sheet for members without phones, which the volunteer enters through the form

### Would build the wrong thing, needs your decision

**1. Two members can both be given the last place in a lane.** Decide whether the form checks that a place is free before confirming; I recommend the database, because then it can. Otherwise people will turn up to full lanes. Worst case: two members submit for the last place in lane 3 at the same moment, both get a confirmation, and one of them arrives on Saturday to find the lane full.
Fix: a confirmation would only be shown once the form has checked that the lane still has a place. With the database the form does this itself. With the spreadsheet, the form must write one row at a time and count the lane before confirming.
*Raised by: buildability*

**2. The Friday tidy-up can delete a booking without anyone noticing.** Decide who is allowed to change the live list while sign-ups are open; I recommend that nobody but the form can. Otherwise a member can lose their place without being told. Worst case: the volunteer sorts or deletes rows on Friday evening while a member is signing up, that booking vanishes, and the member arrives for a lane they no longer hold.
Fix: the volunteer would tidy a copy, or with the database only look at a read-only list, and the copy would replace the live list only after the form closes.
*Raised by: failure cases*

I moved one finding down from this group. The ease-of-use reviewer's "members might find an online page harder than a sheet" was rated as would build the wrong thing. It is now in the worth-fixing group, because it guesses at how members will react and names no specific failure. It is part of finding 4 below.

### Would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call

**3. Anyone with the sheet's link can read every member's phone number.** Decide whether phone numbers go anywhere the link can reach; I recommend they don't. Otherwise members' numbers spread beyond the club. Worst case: a member forwards the link to a friend to show them the timetable, and the friend can now see the phone number of every member who signed up.
Fix: phone numbers would be kept off the shared sheet, or the sheet shared with the volunteer only. With the database, nobody needs to share a link to it.
*Raised by: security*

**4. Some members will be locked out or put off by the online page.** Decide how members who won't or can't use the page still get a lane; I recommend a paper sheet that the volunteer types into the form. Otherwise those members lose their Saturday swim. Worst case: a member without a phone has no way to book and loses their place to members who booked online. Members who are unsure of the page give up and stop booking.
Fix: a paper sheet would stay pinned up for the term and the page would be explained in the club newsletter. The volunteer would enter paper sign-ups through the form so the two lists can't double-book the same place.
*Raised by: ease of use. The "harder than paper" part was moved down from the top group, as noted above.*

**5. Members can't tell whether their booking went through.** Decide what the member sees after pressing the button; I recommend a confirmation naming their lane and time. Otherwise members submit twice. Worst case: the sheet is slow to answer, the page shows nothing, the member submits again, and they hold two places, one of which another member could have had.
Fix: once a booking is saved, the member would see it on screen, with the lane and the time.
*Raised by: failure cases and security (2 reviewers)*

**6. Nothing lets a member cancel a booking.** Decide how a cancelled place is freed; I recommend a cancel link. Otherwise places stay taken by people who aren't coming. Worst case: a member who can't make it has no way to give up their place, so the lane looks full and someone else is turned away.
Fix: each booking would come with a cancel link that frees the place.
*Raised by: buildability*

### Rough edges, nothing to decide

*Raised by: failure cases*
