The spec review (gauntlet) for the telescope loan desk: one finding needs your decision, ten are worth fixing at your discretion, and five are rough edges.

The reviewers kept returning to what happens around a loan. They did not question the core idea of putting the paper ledger online. The finding that matters most is about a telescope coming back. The spec says the loan officer records the telescope as back on the return date with a condition note, and that it then shows as free (AC4). It never says what happens when the officer isn't there. Several other gaps sit around the edges of borrowing:
- Nothing delivers the Objectives' promise that a member can "see where the loan stands".
- AC3 never says whether a waiting-list offer is recorded or how one is closed.
- A cancellation isn't tied to the member who borrowed.
- The 48-hour cancellation window has no stated clock.
- Nothing says whether condition notes are visible to every member.

The failure-cases finding holds up, and it is the most important result. The spec's own Problem is that "nobody can tell afterwards who had a scope when its mirror was scratched", and as AC4 is written that is still how it would fail. The ease-of-use reviewer overreached on its finding of that rank, a member not being told to return the finder cap, because AC5 already sends a reminder listing the case contents. The only real gap left is a loan shorter than two days, where the reminder would arrive before the loan starts. I logged that as worth fixing. The right-problem reviewer suggested lending by the night instead of by start and return date. That is a real alternative, but it depends on a guess about how often loans are for a single night, which the reviewer itself said is unknown. So I downgraded it to "would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call". The fact check refuted "six telescopes": the club's equipment register lists seven. Nothing in the spec depends on that number. I dropped the buildability reviewer's question about whether loan history is stored or derived, because that is a build choice the spec shouldn't make.

My recommendation is to rewrite AC4 so a telescope becomes free only once its condition is recorded, then advance. The repeated gaps, especially the loan-status promise and the recording of waiting-list offers, are worth folding in before planning, but none of them blocks.
