Here is the full detail on the three findings from the spec review that need your decision. Each one starts with what I propose.

**Members are not told what goes back in the case (C1)** — fixed in the spec

I recommend listing what goes back in the case in the email a member gets when they borrow. Without it, the member may first hear about the finder cap when it is already missing. I checked this one myself, and it is narrower than its headline. The spec does tell members, but only in the reminder: "Two days before the return date, the member receives a reminder listing what goes back in the case: the telescope, its eyepieces, and the finder cap" (AC5). The real gap is that they find out only at the end of the loan, and only if that one reminder reaches them.

Worst case: a member types their email address wrong. The borrowing email, "a confirmation email that states the loan and its return date" (AC1), never mentions the case contents, and the reminder (AC5) goes to a dead mailbox. The finder cap stays in the member's garden, and they are asked to replace it.

Fix: `", and what goes back in the case: the telescope, its eyepieces, and the finder cap"` added to the end of the confirmation email's contents in AC1. The member then has the list from the day they borrow, and the reminder (AC5) repeats it.

*Raised by: ease of use. Failure cases reached the same mistyped-mailbox problem from another angle and rated it "would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call".*

**A loan ties up a whole telescope for the full dates asked (C2)** — fixed in the spec

I recommend letting a member say how many nights they want, so a one-night loan holds the scope for one night. Otherwise the club's best scope can sit unused while other members wait for it.

Worst case: the borrowing rule (AC1) takes only a start date, and the system sets the return date. A member who wants the largest scope for one clear Saturday gets it until the return date the system sets, perhaps for the whole weekend or longer. Meanwhile, under the one-member-at-a-time rule, "A telescope is on loan to one member at a time. A second request for the same telescope and dates is refused and the next free start date is offered instead" (AC2), everyone else is refused those nights while the scope sits in a cupboard.

Fix: a member picks the nights they want, and other members are refused only those nights. This rewrites two criteria:

    AC1. A member borrows a telescope by giving a name, an email address, the telescope, a start
    date and the number of nights wanted (one or more), and receives a confirmation email that
    states the loan and its return date.
    AC2. A telescope is on loan to one member at a time, for the nights that member asked for. A
    second request for any of those nights is refused and the next free night is offered instead.

Caveat: the reviewer suggested a rule for adding nights later, and I left it out, because asking for nights up front covers the problem. The reviewer was also unsure how often a loan is really for a single night. If you know that club loans are almost always for a whole weekend, that changes whether this is worth doing.

*Raised by: right problem.*

**A scope left in the cupboard shows as free with no condition note (C3)** — fixed in the spec

I recommend that a telescope shows as free only after someone has recorded its condition. As written, the spec rebuilds the exact problem it set out to fix: a scratched mirror that nobody can trace.

Worst case: the return rule reads "On the return date the loan officer records the telescope as back, with a note of its condition, and the telescope shows as free on the club's loan calendar from that day" (AC4). A build that marks the scope free on the return date, whether or not anyone recorded it, meets that rule. A member leaves the scope in the cupboard on Sunday evening while the loan officer is away. On Monday the calendar shows it as free, and the next member collects it and finds the scratch. The loan history has no condition note for the earlier return, so nobody can say whose loan caused it.

Fix: a returned telescope shows as returned but not yet checked until its condition is recorded. AC4 becomes:

    AC4. When a telescope comes back, the loan officer records it as back with a note of its
    condition. Until that note is recorded, the telescope shows on the club's loan calendar as
    returned and unchecked, not free; it shows as free from the day the note is recorded.

*Raised by: failure cases.*
