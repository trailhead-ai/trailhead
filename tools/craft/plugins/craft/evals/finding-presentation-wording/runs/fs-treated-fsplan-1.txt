**Council review: kiln booking sheet, first build**

maturity: production (basis: stamp). No concerns were waived.

**What the reviewers found.** The findings fall into three groups. First, Task 1 keeps bookings as a queue of `turn` numbers and renumbers that queue on every cancellation. That doesn't fit a sheet where "a kiln holds one firing per day" (AC2), and two reviewers show it can promise one firing to two members. Second, the emails around a booking leave members losing a firing or unsure whether they still have one. The cancellation link cancels as soon as it is opened. The reminder leaves out the 8 am load time. And nobody would notice if the 18:00 job stopped running. Third, some loose ends: a standby offer that never closes, the membership spreadsheet left on a laptop, and a lock reason every member can read.

**How well it holds up.** The buildability and failure-cases reviewers each found the renumbering race on their own, working from steps the plan writes out. That is the strongest finding here. The failure-cases reviewer rated the silent reminder job as worth fixing. I've moved it up to needs your decision, because this spec is at production maturity, and a live job failing with nobody able to see it needs a decision at that level. The ease-of-use reviewer's top finding ("members may find the booking page confusing at first") comes from the reviewer's angle rather than from a gap in the plan. It names no point where a member gets stuck, so I've moved it down. Their reminder finding is stronger than its rating. The spec says the member gets a reminder "listing what must be on the shelves by 8 am" (AC5), and Task 2 as written doesn't do that. It is a one-line fix, so I kept the rating. The security findings are sound. They are rated medium-confidence only because the plan has no sign-in design yet.

**What to do.** Rewrite Task 1 so each booking is one row for a kiln and a day. A second booking for that day is refused and offered the next free day, and the turn number goes. Give the 18:00 job a signal that it ran, and add the 8 am line to the reminder. Make the cancellation link open a page that asks the member to confirm, then confirms the cancellation. Close a member's standby entry when they are offered a day. Delete the spreadsheet once the load is checked. Only the first two need your decision before this can go to build.

**Would build the wrong thing, needs your decision**

- **Should a booking be one row per kiln and day instead of a numbered queue?** I recommend yes. As planned, one firing can be promised to two members.
  Worst case: a member cancels just as the 18:00 reminder job is reading bookings. Task 1 subtracts one from every later `turn`, the job sees two rows with the same number, and both members get a reminder for the same firing. One of them turns up and loads a kiln they don't hold.
  Fix: members would hold a day rather than a place in line, and a second booking for the same kiln and day would be refused with the next free day offered. This rewrites Task 1 and changes how Tasks 2 and 5 sort bookings: by booked day, with no stored turn.
  *Raised by: buildability, failure cases*

- **Should the 18:00 reminder job tell someone when it doesn't run?** I recommend yes. Otherwise reminders stop and the first sign is a missed firing.
  Worst case: one evening the job doesn't run. No member gets a reminder, and nobody finds out until members turn up unprepared and lose their firings.
  Fix: in Task 2, email the technician when the job finishes, and send another if it hasn't finished by 19:00.
  *Raised by: failure cases. The reviewer rated this "worth fixing, your call"; I moved it here because this spec is at production maturity.*

**Would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call**

- **Should the cancellation link ask before it cancels?** I recommend yes. As planned, opening the email is enough to lose the booking.
  Worst case: a mail scanner follows links, or the member forwards the email, and the firing is cancelled without the member knowing. A member who clicks by mistake gets no word on whether the booking still stands.
  Fix: in Task 3, the link opens a page asking the member to confirm, then shows and emails a one-line confirmation of the cancellation.
  *Raised by: security, ease of use*
