**Spec review: a waiting list for allotment plots. Round 1. Maturity: production (basis: the spec's own stamp). No concerns were waived.**

The review found two problems, and both are about the people already on the list. First, an offer can be lost without anyone noticing. The spec sends every offer by email and says "the offer stays open for 14 days" (AC2). It also says someone who doesn't answer "goes to the back of the queue" (AC3). Nothing covers an email that never arrives. Second, nothing brings the existing paper list across. The spec only describes a person who joins "by giving a name, an email address and the site they want" (AC1). The people who have waited longest would have to join again at the back. Those without email could not join at all. Some smaller gaps sit alongside these: nothing says how a freed plot gets into the system, how someone checks their place later, or whether a person can wait for more than one site.

The lost-offer finding is the strongest. The failure-cases and ease-of-use reviewers reached it separately, each without seeing the other's work. It is concrete: a mistyped address leaves the plot empty for two weeks, then quietly sends someone who waited years to the back. The right-problem reviewer's claim about the existing list rests on a guess it admits: how many older members have no email. I checked the spec and the finding stands without that guess. The spec says the warden "rings people" today, so the current list is a phone list. At production maturity, moving existing people across in their order is a decision for you, not a rough edge. The right-problem reviewer's second point said that steps 1–5 are more than the complaint needs. That overreaches: the Problem names "nobody can see where they stand" as its own complaint, so I dropped it. The fact check found the list holds 412 names, per the committee minutes of 3 March, not "about 340". That correction is folded into the second fix. It also could not confirm that the warden actually keeps to the order. Copying the paper order across makes that order official, so it is worth a word with the warden.

Both problems can be fixed by editing this spec, so neither needs to send the work back. I have written five new criteria in full. Three cover a lost or unanswered offer: pass it on straight away, send a reminder, and tell the person when it lapses. Two cover the existing list: enter it in its paper order, and allow people with only a phone number. The eight smaller items below are worth a look, but none of them blocks the spec.
