## What the panel found

The panel found that the risk sits in how the spreadsheet would be run, not in whether a spreadsheet is good enough. Two reviewers independently found ways a member could turn up on Saturday for a place they don't actually hold. In one, the form and the spreadsheet both take bookings, so two people can grab the last place in a lane at the same moment. In the other, the volunteer's Friday tidy-up sorts or deletes rows while sign-ups are still open. Around those two problems are smaller ones:

- nobody owns cancellations
- members get no clear confirmation, so they submit twice
- phone numbers are readable by anyone who has the sheet's link
- members who won't or can't book online have no way in

## Whether it holds

The buildability and failure-cases findings both hold, and the club's own numbers make them urgent. About 60 members want 40 places, so the last places will fill by a race every week. The security finding is real but depends on who receives the link, which is why it was raised as worth fixing rather than as a decision. The ease-of-use reviewer's main concern, that members might find the page harder than paper, names no concrete failure and guesses at how members will behave. I moved it from "would build the wrong thing, needs your decision" to "would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call". Their other point is concrete: members without a phone are shut out. The migration and rollback points are true but minor for a one-term trial.

## What to do

Both serious problems disappear if the form is the only thing that writes bookings and checks a lane's count before confirming. A small database behind the form gives you that from the start. A spreadsheet can only manage it if the form is its sole writer and the volunteer works on a copy after sign-ups close. So I recommend the small database. Alongside it:

- add a cancel link
- show the member their booking on screen after they save it
- keep phone numbers out of anything shared by link
- keep the paper sheet pinned up for the term, so phone-less members can still book and the club can fall back to paper if the trial is dropped

## Findings

### Would build the wrong thing, needs your decision

**1. Do you let the form be the only thing that takes bookings, so two members can't both get the last place in a lane? I recommend yes. Without it, someone will turn up to a full lane holding a confirmation.**
With the form and the shared spreadsheet both writing bookings, two members can book the last place at the same moment and both get a confirmation. With 60 members chasing 40 places, the last places will be contested most weeks.
Fix: a member could never be confirmed into a full lane. The form saves one booking at a time and checks the lane's count before confirming. A small database behind the form does this naturally.
*Raised by: buildability*

**2. Do you stop the Friday tidy-up from touching live bookings? I recommend yes. Otherwise a member's booking can vanish without them ever being told.**
If the volunteer sorts or deletes rows while members are still signing up, a booking disappears silently, and the member arrives for a lane they no longer hold.
Fix: bookings would never disappear mid-week. The tidy-up runs on a copy, which is swapped in only after the sign-up form has closed. This is unnecessary if bookings live in a database the volunteer doesn't edit by hand.
*Raised by: failure cases*

### Would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call

**3. Keep the paper sheet pinned up beside the page for the term, so members who won't or can't book online aren't shut out.**
A member who doesn't use a phone for sign-ups cannot book at all. Others may find the page harder than paper. That second concern was downgraded because nobody has asked the members, and it names no concrete failure.
Fix: every member would still have a way to book. Keep the paper sheet up for the term and explain the page in the club newsletter.
*Raised by: ease of use*

**4. Keep phone numbers off the shared sheet. Anyone the link is passed to can currently read every member's number.**
The page collects names and phone numbers. The sheet is shared by link, so every phone number is visible to whoever holds the link.
Fix: members' phone numbers would no longer travel with the link. Leave them off the sheet, or share the sheet with the volunteer only.
*Raised by: security*

**5. Decide who removes a booking when a member cancels. Right now nobody does, so cancelled places stay taken.**
A member who can't come has no way to free their place, so the lane looks full when it isn't.
Fix: a cancelled place would open up for someone else. "give the form a cancel link that clears the row."
*Raised by: buildability*

**6. Show members their booking once it's saved, so they don't submit twice when the page is slow.**
If saving takes a while, the page gives no sign the booking went through, so members submit again and take two places.
Fix: members would see their booking on screen straight after saving.
*Raised by: failure cases*

### Rough edges, nothing to decide

**7. When the trial ends, the bookings will have to be copied by hand into whatever replaces the sheet.** This is migration and backfill. It is only a rough edge because this is a prototype trial.
Fix: the column layout written down in the trial's notes, so the copy is straightforward.
*Raised by: buildability*

**8. Going back to paper midway through the term would mean telling members and re-entering that week's bookings.** This is rollback and reversibility. It is only a rough edge because this is a prototype trial. Keeping the paper sheet up, as in finding 3, also covers this.
Fix: the paper sheet kept pinned up for at least the first week.
*Raised by: failure cases*

**9. The confirmation message gives members no way to tell it's real.**
Fix: "The confirmation message could name the lane and time, so a member can tell it is real."
*Raised by: security*

**10. The sign-up button says "submit".**
Fix: `"book my lane"` instead of `"submit"` on the sign-up button, so members know what pressing it does.
*Raised by: ease of use*
