**Council review: kiln booking sheet, first build**

`maturity: production (basis: stamp)`. No concern was waived.

**What the reviews found.** Two problems run through the reports. The first is how Task 1 keeps its place in line. Every booking gets a `turn` number, and cancelling a booking renumbers every later one. Two reviewers showed independently that this can tell two members the same firing is theirs. The second is that the plan's emails and links do less than the spec asks. The cancel link in Task 3 acts as soon as the email is opened. It doesn't tell the member the booking is gone, and it doesn't take the member who was offered the day off the standby list. The 18:00 reminder in Task 2 leaves out the 8 am shelf deadline that AC5 requires, and nobody would notice if the reminder job stopped running. Smaller points cover the membership spreadsheet left on a laptop, lock reasons that every member can read, and some tidying up.

**Whether it holds.** The renumbering finding is solid. Task 1 describes the renumbering step itself, and AC2 already says a kiln holds one firing a day, so a booking only needs its kiln and day. The ease-of-use reviewer's top-rated finding said only that members "may find the booking page confusing" and named no moment where anyone gets stuck. I lowered it to "worth fixing, your call". The failure-cases reviewer rated the silent reminder job "worth fixing". At production maturity, a failure nobody can see is rated "needs your decision", so I raised it. The point about the word "release" refers to text the plan doesn't contain, so it is a guess about a screen that hasn't been built. The reviews also missed some things. No task has a test contract. No task refuses a second booking for the same day and offers the next free day (AC2). No task enforces the 24-hour cancellation limit (AC3); a reviewer noticed only the missing boundary test.

**What to do.** Rewrite Task 1 so a booking is identified by its kiln and day, with no turn number. Have Task 2 alert the technician when the reminder job finishes, and when it hasn't run by 19:00. Change Task 3's cancel link so it opens a confirmation page, then confirms the cancellation and takes the offered member off the standby list. While editing, add the 8 am line to the reminder, the AC2 refusal and AC3 cutoff, and a test contract for each task.

### Would build the wrong thing, needs your decision

**1. Approve replacing the `turn` number with the booked kiln and day.** Otherwise a cancellation can hand one firing to two members.
A cancellation renumbers every later booking. If that happens while the 18:00 reminder job is reading the table, two rows end up with the same `turn`. Both members are told the firing is theirs, and one of them turns up to load a kiln they don't hold.
Fix: a booking would be identified by its kiln and day, and a cancellation would never move anyone else's booking. This rewrites Task 1 and changes how Tasks 2 and 3 read bookings.
*Raised by: buildability, failure cases*

**2. Decide whether the reminder job must report that it ran.** Today it can stop without anyone knowing.
If the 18:00 job fails, reminders stop arriving and nobody finds out until a member misses a firing.
Fix: the technician would get an email when the job finishes, and another if it hasn't run by 19:00. This adds a step to Task 2.
*Raised by: failure cases (raised from "worth fixing", because a failure nobody can see needs a decision at production maturity)*

### Would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call

**3. Make the cancel link open a confirmation page instead of cancelling straight away.** A forwarded email or a mail scanner can cancel someone's firing.
The link in Task 3 cancels as soon as it is opened, and nothing ties it to the member it was sent to. A mail scanner that follows links, or a forwarded email, cancels the booking without the member doing anything.
Fix: the link would open a page that asks the member to confirm. This changes Task 3.
*Raised by: security*

**4. Tell the member when a cancellation has gone through.** A member who clicked by mistake can't tell whether their booking still stands.
Fix: `show and email a one-line confirmation` after a cancellation, in Task 3.
*Raised by: ease of use*

**5. Take the member who was offered a freed day off the standby list.** Otherwise the same member is offered one day after another.
Task 3 offers the freed day to the first member on the standby list but leaves them there. The next cancellation offers that same member a second day.
Fix: making the offer would close that member's standby row. This changes Task 3.
*Raised by: buildability*

**6. Give the 8 am shelf deadline in the reminder email.** AC5 requires it, and Task 2 leaves it out.
Members who don't know the deadline arrive at 9, find the kiln already packed, and lose their firing.
Fix: "add a sentence to the reminder email giving the 8 am load time", in Task 2.
*Raised by: ease of use*

**7. Delete the membership spreadsheet once the load is checked.** It holds 90 names and email addresses and will stay on the technician's laptop.
Fix: the spreadsheet would be deleted once the load is verified, and the plan's launch note would say so.
*Raised by: security*

**8. Decide whether the booking page needs help text.** The reviewer didn't name a moment where a member gets stuck.
Fix: "add a help page".
*Raised by: ease of use (lowered from "needs your decision": it describes no actual failure)*

### Rough edges, nothing to decide

**9. Lock reasons are visible to every member.** A reason the technician types appears on the calendar page and may include personal details. I recommend telling technicians not to put personal details in the reason. *Raised by: security*

**10. The 24-hour cancellation limit has no boundary test.** No test covers a cancellation made exactly 24 hours before the booked day. The plan doesn't enforce the limit at all yet, so add the rule and its test together. *Raised by: failure cases*

**11. Two tasks format dates separately.** Tasks 2 and 5 should share one date-formatting helper. *Raised by: buildability*

**12. "Release" may read as jargon.** The reviewer says the calendar calls a freed day a "release", but the plan doesn't use that word. Keep it in mind when the page is written. *Raised by: ease of use*
