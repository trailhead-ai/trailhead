**Spec review: a waiting list for allotment plots.** Round 1, all 8 passes returned. Maturity: production (basis: stamp). No stand-downs.

The reviewers agree on one main problem: the spec trusts email to carry every offer, and it never says what happens when email fails. If the offer email bounces or nobody reads it, a person sits on "an offer that stays open for 14 days" (AC2). Then they "go to the back of the queue" (AC3) without ever knowing they had an offer. Separately, the spec treats joining as something a person does online with an email address (AC1). It never says how the people already on the paper list get into the new queue in their current order. The rest of the findings are gaps rather than wrong turns. Nothing lets a person check their place after the first email. Nothing says who marks a plot as freed, or what happens when a site's queue is empty. Two choices are left open: whether a person can wait on more than one site's queue, and when the 14 days start counting.

The undelivered-offer finding is the strongest one. The failure-cases and ease-of-use reviewers reached it separately, and the spec's own text produces it without any guessing about how users behave. The right-problem reviewer was also right that some people can't take part by email. I can't confirm its claim that "a large part" of the list has no email, and I haven't relied on it. The exclusion follows from AC1 whatever the share is. The share only changes how much it hurts. On my own reading, the spec also says nothing about moving the existing list across. At production maturity, moving existing data across is graded as serious, so I've added that to the same finding. The fact check found that the spec's "about 340" is wrong: the committee minutes of 3 March record 412 names. That changes no decisions, but it's corrected in the same edit. The right problem reviewer's suggestion that only the history (AC6) is really needed is the lens overreaching. The Objectives ask for all three things.

My recommendation is to fix both by editing the criteria now. Neither needs rethinking. Undelivered offers move on straight away without costing the person their place. A reminder goes out before the offer lapses. Existing names are carried across in their order, and a warden can enter someone by phone. The edits below are fully drafted.
