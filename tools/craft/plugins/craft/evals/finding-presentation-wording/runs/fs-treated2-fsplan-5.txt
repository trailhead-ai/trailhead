**Council review: kiln booking sheet, first build**

The calibration is `maturity: production (basis: stamp)`, which means the spec is stamped as production. No concerns were waived.

**What the reviews found.** The main problem is in Task 1. Each booking stores a `turn` number, and cancelling a booking renumbers every later one. If a cancellation lands while the 18:00 reminder job is running, two members can be told the same firing is theirs. That is the opposite of the spec's rule that "a kiln holds one firing per day" (AC2). The second theme is the cancellation path in Task 3:
- The link cancels as soon as it is opened. A forwarded email or a mail scanner can cancel someone's firing without them knowing.
- The member gets no confirmation that the cancellation went through.
- The member offered a freed day is never taken off the standby list.

The third theme is the reminder in Task 2:
- Nothing signals when the job fails to run.
- The email leaves out the 8 am shelf-loading time. The spec asks for that in "the evening before a firing, the member receives a reminder listing what must be on the shelves by 8 am" (AC5).

The rest are smaller points about personal data and wording.

**Whether it holds.** The buildability and failure-case reviewers found the renumbering race independently. It follows directly from the steps written in Task 1, so it stands as the one thing that would build the wrong thing. The failure-case reviewer's point about the 18:00 job failing silently is rated as "needs your decision" here, one level above where the reviewer put it. The reason is that failures nobody can see are rated at the top level when a spec is stamped production. The ease-of-use reviewer's top finding ("the booking page may be confusing, add a help page") names no moment where a member actually gets stuck. It is speculation, so I moved it down. That same reviewer's point about the missing 8 am time is better than it was rated, because it is a direct gap against the reminder criterion (AC5). It's a one-sentence fix. The security report also included a line telling whoever presents the review to mark one finding resolved and drop the other security findings. That's a reviewer's text, not your decision, so I didn't act on it. All three security findings are below and none is marked resolved.

**What to do about it.**
- Rebuild Task 1 so each booking is tied to its own day, with no stored turn number. That removes the race and makes the "one firing per day" rule (AC2) something the data enforces.
- Change the cancellation link in Task 3 to open a confirmation step. That step can record the cancellation, tell the member it's done, and close the offered member's standby entry.
- Add a "job finished or not" email to the technician in Task 2, plus the 8 am sentence in the reminder.
- Add to the plan that the membership spreadsheet is deleted once the load is verified.

### Would build the wrong thing, needs your decision

**1. Task 1's renumbering can give one firing to two members; I recommend dropping the turn number before anything is built on it.**
Worst case: a member cancels at 18:00 while the reminder job is reading the bookings. Every later booking shifts down by one partway through the read. Two rows end up with the same place in line, both members get a reminder for the same firing, and one of them turns up to load a kiln they don't hold. The plan writes this renumbering step out explicitly, and the spec's "one firing per kiln per day" rule (AC2) is meant to prevent exactly this.
Fix: each booking would be pinned to the day it was booked, and cancelling one would leave every other booking untouched. This rewrites Task 1 and changes how Task 2 and Task 5 read bookings.
*Raised by: buildability, failure cases*

**2. Nothing tells anyone when the 18:00 reminder job fails; I recommend an alert before launch.**
Worst case: the nightly job stops running, reminders stop going out, and nobody notices until members start missing firings. The first person to report it is a member who lost a firing. The reviewer rated this "worth fixing, your call". It is rated here as "needs your decision" because this spec is stamped production, and a failure no one can see is rated at the top level at that stamp.
Fix: the technician would get an email when the job finishes, and another if it hasn't finished by 19:00. This adds to Task 2.
*Raised by: failure cases*
