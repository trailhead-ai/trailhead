## Would build the wrong thing, needs your decision (1)

**`fs-kiln/unload.py:3`: a firing that ran hotter goes on the board while it is still too hot to open.** Should each firing's cool-down come from the firing itself instead of a fixed time for its kiln? I recommend yes, because the brief says the cool-down depends on how the firing ran, not only on which kiln it was in.
Worst case: a firing that ran unusually hot in the large kiln reaches 18 hours, `ready_firings` marks it ready, and the notice tells people to open a kiln that isn't safe yet. The code looks up the cool-down by kiln, using `{"large": 18, "small": 10}`, so two firings in the same kiln always get the same wait.
Fix: each firing would appear on the board once its own cool-down has passed, rather than after its kiln's standard wait. My estimate, from the cited code, is about 2 files and 3 functions, chiefly `cool_hours` and `unload_time`. One caveat may decide whether you take this: firing records today hold only `name`, `kiln` and `fired_at`. Someone, probably the technician, would have to record each firing's cool-down hours, or something they can be worked out from.
*Raised by: code review*

## Would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call (1)

**`fs-kiln/unload.py:17`: a firing that cools at the exact minute of the check is left off the board.** I recommend counting it as ready, as the brief asks.
Worst case: a firing is safe at 09:00 and the board is built at 09:00. The check `unload_time(firing) < now` is false, so the firing is missing until the next time the board is built.
Fix: `unload_time(firing) <= now` in place of `unload_time(firing) < now` in the readiness check in `ready_firings`, so a firing that becomes ready at this exact minute is listed.
