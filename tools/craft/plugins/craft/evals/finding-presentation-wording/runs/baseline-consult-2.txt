**maturity: production (basis: requested)**. There are no `stand-down:` or `waiver-not-recognised:` lines, so every concern is rated at production severity.

## What the council found

The panel kept returning to two problems with letting tenants swap plots directly. The first is that the plot records can go wrong. Two swaps that name the same plot can both be confirmed, a half-confirmed swap has no defined end, and nothing records past swaps, so a later dispute can't be settled. The second is the committee's promise that nobody on the 340-person waiting list is passed over. Direct swaps let tenants decide among themselves who ends up on which plot, and the warden no longer sees it happen. A smaller point ran through both: the warden handles about six swaps a year, and the current hand process works.

The double-swap problem is real, and Builder and Reliability found it separately. It follows directly from a design where each tenant confirms against what they saw when they started. Six swaps a year makes a collision rare. When one happens, though, a tenant arrives at a plot they thought was theirs and finds someone else on it, so it stays Critical. Security's waiting-list concern holds, but its mechanism is loose. A swap between two sitting tenants doesn't by itself admit anyone. The real route is a tenant who is about to leave "swapping" a good plot to a friend first, so that the plot never reaches the waiting list. Its suggested fix, warden approval only for moves to a different site, assumes several sites that the question never mentions. Advocate's Critical, that tenants "might feel" the process is unfair, names no concrete failure. It is a guess about how people will feel, so I've downgraded it to Important. Advocate's other point is the stronger one: most tenants aren't online, and the volume is tiny.

My recommendation is to keep swaps with the warden. Six a year doesn't justify a system whose main new failure is two tenants holding the same plot. The warden also already acts as the check that stops a good plot going to a friend instead of to the waiting list. If the society still wants the form, offer it alongside the warden route, not instead of it, and build it with four things:
- one record of who holds each plot
- a check at the second confirmation that both tenants still hold the plots named
- expiry for a swap that only one tenant has confirmed
- a permanent swap history and warden sign-off on every swap

The waiting-list spec explicitly sets swaps aside as a Non-Goal, so this work needs its own spec rather than being added to that one.

## Findings

### Critical

**Two tenants can be given the same plot.**
The form checks each swap against the plots the tenants held when they started it. If two swaps naming the same plot are confirmed close together, both are applied. One tenant then turns up to find a stranger on the plot they swapped for.
Fix: keep one record of who holds each plot, and apply a swap only if both tenants still hold the named plots when the second confirmation arrives.
*Raised by 2 lenses: Builder, Reliability.*

### Important

**A swap could keep a good plot away from the waiting list.**
A tenant who is about to give up their plot could first swap it with a friend for a worse one, then give up the worse one. The good plot never reaches the 340 people waiting, and money may change hands. That breaks the committee's promise that nobody on the list is passed over, and with direct swaps no one with authority sees it happen.
Fix: require the warden's sign-off on every swap, not only moves between sites. The reviewer's suggested scope assumes several sites, which the question doesn't mention.
*Security.*

**Replacing the warden adds work for tenants and solves very little.**
The warden handles about six swaps a year and it works. Most tenants aren't online, so a form-only route makes swapping harder for them.
Fix: keep the warden route. If the form is built, offer it as a second way to swap, not a replacement.
*Advocate.*

**There is no record of past swaps.**
If a tenant disputes a swap a year later, nothing shows who agreed to what or when.
Fix: write every swap to the same history the waiting list uses.
*Reliability.*

**A swap that only one tenant has confirmed has no defined end.**
If the second tenant never confirms, the swap sits open indefinitely and could block or confuse later swaps of the same plots.
Fix: expire an unconfirmed swap after a set period, such as seven days, and record why that period was chosen.
*Builder, Reliability. Reliability noted that seven days is arbitrary.*

**Tenants may see the swap process as unfair to people on the waiting list.**
This was raised as Critical and downgraded: it guesses at how people will feel and describes no concrete failure.
Fix: explain on the society's site how swaps work and how they relate to the waiting list.
*Advocate.*

### Minor

- **The confirmation email should name both plots**, so a tenant can tell the request is genuine. *Security.*
- **Call the form "swap my plot", not "reallocate".** *Advocate.*
- **The form could reuse the waiting list's email templates.** *Builder.*
