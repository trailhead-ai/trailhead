**Gauntlet: spec "a waiting list for allotment plots"** (round 1)
`maturity: production (basis: stamp)`, with no stand-downs and no waivers. Facts: 2 of 4 confirmed, 1 refuted, 1 unverifiable.

**What the passes found.** Two separate gaps came up, and both affect the people the spec most needs to serve. The first is the people already on the paper list. The spec never says how they get into the online queue in their current order. Joining also requires an email address: AC1 says "a person joins the waiting list for one site by giving a name, an email address and the site they want". So anyone the warden can only phone today has no way to keep their place. The second is an offer email that never arrives. The spec says the person at the front "is sent an offer by email, and the offer stays open for 14 days" (AC2). It also says that if the offer is not accepted, "the person who did not answer goes to the back of the queue" (AC3). With a mistyped address, the plot sits empty for two weeks and then the longest waiter is moved to the back without ever being told. The other findings are smaller holes. Nothing says who marks a plot as freed. Nothing says what happens when a site's queue is empty. Members have no way to "see their place" after the first email, although the Objectives promise it.

**Whether it holds.** Both Criticals hold. The undelivered-offer finding is the strongest signal in this run. Reliability and Advocate reached it on their own, one through bounced mail and one through the person's experience. The premise pass was right that the email-only join is a problem. It was wrong that the cause is a guess about members' ages. The real cause is that the spec has no step for moving the existing list across, and at production maturity, migration and backfill is a Critical concern. The phone fallback is not speculative either: the warden phones people today, so the society already holds phone numbers. The premise pass went too far in arguing that the goal is only to check the order and that AC1 to AC5 are more than needed. The Problem lists "nobody can see where they stand" as a complaint in its own right, so I am not taking that up. The fact pass refuted the list size: the minutes of 3 March record 412 names, not about 340. That correction goes into C1's edit because C1 is about the same list.

**What to do.** Add one criterion for moving the paper list across in its current order, with phone-only entries that the warden offers by phone. Correct the list size. Add a rule for undelivered offers, a day-10 reminder, and a message when an offer lapses. Every finding can be fixed by editing this spec, so nothing needs `revise`.

**Recommended outcome:** advance to `ready` this round once you accept. No `craft/supersedes` label is set.
