**Members are not told what goes back in the case (C1)**
Proposed outcome: fixed in the spec.

Should members be told what goes back in the case when they borrow, and not only near the end of the loan? I recommend yes, because otherwise a member who never sees the late reminder returns the case without its finder cap and is asked to replace it.

Worst case: the spec says "A member borrows a telescope by giving a name, an email address, the telescope and a start date, and receives a confirmation email that states the loan and its return date" (AC1). The only other place the contents are listed is "Two days before the return date, the member receives a reminder listing what goes back in the case: the telescope, its eyepieces, and the finder cap" (AC5). A member packs and unpacks the case at the start of the loan, when nothing has told them what belongs in it. If they mistyped their email, the reminder in that two-day reminder (AC5) bounces without anyone knowing. Either way the finder cap stays at home and the member gets the bill.

Fix: `", and lists what goes back in the case: the telescope, its eyepieces, and the finder cap"` added to the end of the confirmation email (AC1), so the list reaches the member at the moment they open the case.

*Raised by: ease of use. Failure cases reached the same harm separately (a bounced reminder means the case goes back without its finder cap) and logged it as worth fixing.*

**A loan ties up a whole telescope for all the dates asked (C2)**
Proposed outcome: needs a rewrite (`record-only`).

Should the club lend a telescope by the night instead of from a start date to a return date? I recommend deciding this before planning, because the loan unit shapes every borrowing criterion and is costly to change once built.

Worst case: the spec ties each loan to a telescope and a start date (AC1), and says "A telescope is on loan to one member at a time. A second request for the same telescope and dates is refused and the next free start date is offered instead" (AC2). A member who wants the largest scope for one clear night books it for a weekend. Other members are refused for that whole span and offered dates weeks away, while the scope sits unused in a garage for most of the booking. That is the same "two members want the same scope" complaint the spec was written to fix.

Fix: members would book a telescope for the night or nights they want, and could add nights. A clash would then cost one night, not a weekend. This rewrites all three Borrowing criteria (AC1, AC2, AC3) and changes the return date that recording the return (AC4) and the reminder (AC5) are counted from. Caveat: this is only worth doing if single-night loans are common. The right-problem reviewer had no evidence on that and rated its own confidence medium. If you know most loans are multi-night trips, answer this finding and I'll re-adjudicate it.

*Raised by: right problem.*

**A scope left in the cupboard shows as free with no condition note (C3)**
Proposed outcome: fixed in the spec.

Should a returned telescope stay off the calendar until someone has checked it? I recommend yes. Otherwise the scratched-mirror problem the spec opens with survives the build.

Worst case: the spec says "On the return date the loan officer records the telescope as back, with a note of its condition, and the telescope shows as free on the club's loan calendar from that day" (AC4). A member drops the scope in the cupboard while the loan officer is away. It shows as free with no note, the next member finds the scratch, and the history behind "The loan history lists every loan, cancellation, return and waiting-list offer for a telescope, newest first, with the date and the condition note" (AC6) has no note that could say who had it.

Fix: `"The telescope shows on the club's loan calendar as returned and not yet checked until the loan officer records a note of its condition, and as free only from then."` replaces the last clause of that return-recording criterion (AC4), so no scope can be lent out again before someone has looked at it.
