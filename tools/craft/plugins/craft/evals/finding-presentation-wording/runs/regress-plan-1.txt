**Council review: allotment waiting list, first build**

maturity: production (basis: stamp). No concern was waived by a Non-Goal.

**What the reviews found.** Two parts of the plan look unsafe. The first is how the queue keeps its order. Task 1 stores a `place` number for each person and renumbers every later row when someone leaves. Task 2's 02:00 offer job reads that same number. The second is Task 4's one-off load of the paper list: about 340 people imported from the warden's laptop with no preview and no way to undo it. Around those two problems are smaller gaps:
- Nothing tells anyone if the nightly job stops running.
- The offer link accepts a plot as soon as the link is opened.
- A plot granted out of turn leaves the person still in the queue.
- The emails don't tell people what they stand to lose.

**Whether the findings hold.** The queue-order finding is the strongest. The buildability and failure-cases reviews reached it separately, and it follows directly from the steps Task 1 writes out. The import finding is also sound, and this spec is at production maturity, so an import with no undo needs your decision. The failure-cases review rated the silent nightly job as worth fixing. I've moved it up to needs your decision, because at production maturity, failure that nobody can see sits at that level. The ease-of-use review's call for a help page doesn't name a moment where anyone gets stuck. It reflects that review's focus more than a flaw in the plan, so I've moved it from "would build the wrong thing, needs your decision" to "would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call". Its two email findings are real. The security review flagged its own medium confidence because the plan has no sign-in design yet. Two gaps fall outside all four reports and should be fixed in the same revision:
- No task carries a test contract.
- The warden screen doesn't say how the system knows the user is a warden.

**What to do.** Stop storing a place number. Order each site's queue by a queue timestamp. Set the timestamp when a person joins. Reset it to "now" when an offer lapses, which keeps "the person who did not answer goes to the back of the queue" true (AC3). Close the row when the person leaves or is granted a plot. A person's place is then always calculated, so "the people behind them each move up one place" holds with no renumbering (AC5). Run the import under a batch id: preview it first, refuse to load the same batch twice, and allow a bad batch to be removed whole. Have the nightly job email the warden when it finishes, and email again if it hasn't finished by 03:00. Then take the worth-fixing items as one pass over the emails and the warden screen.

**Would build the wrong thing, needs your decision**

1. **Should the queue keep its order without a stored place number? I recommend yes. Otherwise one freed plot can be offered to two people.** Task 1 renumbers every later `place` when someone leaves. If someone leaves while the 02:00 job is running, two rows can both hold place 1, and both people get an offer email for the same plot.
   Fix: order each site's queue by a queue timestamp. Reset the timestamp on a lapse, so that "goes to the back of the queue" (AC3) still holds. Calculate a person's place when showing it, and never store it.
   *Raised by: buildability, failure cases*

2. **Should the paper-list import be previewable and undoable before it runs? I recommend yes. These 340 people are the whole starting queue.** If the import runs twice, everyone has two rows and gets two offers. If it stops halfway, nothing records where it stopped, so some people are missing from the queue and nobody can tell which.
   Fix: import under a batch id. Show a preview before writing. Refuse to load a batch id that has already been loaded. Allow a bad batch to be removed whole.
   *Raised by: failure cases*

3. **Should a missed nightly run alert someone? I recommend yes. Otherwise offers can stop with nobody noticing.** If the 02:00 job fails or never starts, no offers go out and no lapses are processed. The first sign is someone writing in weeks later to ask why they've heard nothing. The failure-cases review rated this worth fixing. I raised it because production maturity puts failures nobody can see at this level.
   Fix: email the warden when the job finishes, and email again if it hasn't finished by 03:00.
   *Raised by: failure cases*

**Would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call**

4. **The offer link should open a confirmation page, not accept on open.** A forwarded email, or a mail scanner that follows links before the person reads the message, would accept the plot for them.
   Fix: make the link open a page where the person confirms acceptance.
   *Raised by: security*

5. **A plot granted out of turn should take that person off the queue.** Task 3 records the grant, but the person's queue row stays open, so the nightly job would later offer them a second plot.
   Fix: have the grant close that person's queue row (and record the departure in the history).
   *Raised by: buildability*

6. **The offer email should warn people that ignoring it moves them to the back of the queue.** As written, people will lose their place without knowing they were at risk.
   Fix: state the 14-day deadline and its consequence in the first paragraph of the offer email.
   *Raised by: ease of use*

7. **Leaving the queue should confirm that it worked.** Someone who clicked by mistake can't tell whether they are still on the list.
   Fix: show a one-line confirmation on screen and send the same line by email.
   *Raised by: ease of use*

8. **Delete the spreadsheet once the import is checked.** The spreadsheet holds 340 names and email addresses and will stay on the warden's laptop.
   Fix: add a step to Task 4 to delete the file after the import is verified, and record that it was deleted.
   *Raised by: security*

9. **A help page is worth considering, but nobody has yet pointed to a moment where someone gets stuck.** I moved this from "would build the wrong thing, needs your decision" to "would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call" because it doesn't describe a concrete failure. It is a guess about how people will react.
   Fix: decide this once the screens exist and you can see where people actually get stuck.
   *Raised by: ease of use*

**Rough edges, nothing to decide**

10. The reason a warden types for an out-of-turn grant appears on a history page that every committee member can read, and it may contain personal details. I recommend a short note on the warden screen telling wardens to keep reasons factual. *Raised by: security*
11. No test covers an offer that is exactly 14 days old. I recommend adding that boundary to Task 2's tests. *Raised by: failure cases*
12. The history page uses the word "lapse", which reads as jargon. "Offer expired" is clearer. *Raised by: ease of use*
13. Tasks 2 and 5 both format dates for display. They should share one helper. *Raised by: buildability*
