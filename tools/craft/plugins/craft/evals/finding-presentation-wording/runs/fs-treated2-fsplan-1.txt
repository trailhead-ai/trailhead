**Council review: kiln booking sheet, first build**

`maturity: production (basis: stamp)`. No concern was waived.

**What the reviews found.** Two problems sit in the parts the spec cares about most. First, Task 1 stores a `turn` number for each booking and renumbers every later booking when one is cancelled. That puts a booking's place in a number that changes, and a cancellation can collide with the 18:00 reminder job. It also works against the spec's simpler rule that a kiln holds one firing per day (AC2). Second, the reminder job in Task 2 can stop without anyone noticing, and the reminder itself leaves out the 8 am load time. Task 3's one-click cancellation link has its own cluster of problems. Opening the link is enough to cancel, so a forwarded email or a mail scanner can do it. The member gets no confirmation afterwards. The member who was offered a freed day stays on the standby list. The rest are smaller: the membership spreadsheet left on the technician's laptop, lock reasons that every member can read, and some tidying.

**How well the findings hold up.** The buildability and failure-cases reviews found the turn-number collision separately, and it follows directly from steps the plan writes out, so it stands. The failure-cases review filed the silent reminder job as "your call". At production maturity, a failure nobody would see is rated "needs your decision", so I have raised it to that group. The security review's points about the cancellation link and the spreadsheet are concrete. The ease-of-use review's top finding, that members may find the booking page confusing, names no screen and no moment where someone gets stuck. I have moved it down to "your call". Its 8 am point is real and is a gap against AC5. Its point about the word "release" is a lens artifact, because that word appears nowhere in the plan or the spec. None of the reviews mentioned three things I see in the plan itself. No task has a test contract. Task 3 never states the 24-hour cancellation cutoff that AC3 requires. Task 1 never says how a second booking for the same kiln and day is refused and the next free day offered (AC2).

**What to do about it.** Rewrite Task 1 so each booking is keyed by kiln and day and ordered by its date, with no turn number. That fixes the collision and expresses AC2 directly. Add a "the reminders ran" signal to Task 2, plus the 8 am line. Change the cancellation in Task 3 to "confirm, then cancel, then tell the member", enforce the 24-hour cutoff there, and close the standby row when an offer goes out. Add a test contract to every task. Add a line about deleting the spreadsheet. Everything else can wait.

### Would build the wrong thing, needs your decision

**1. Should bookings keep a changing `turn` number? I recommend no: order them by booked day. Otherwise two members can be told the same firing is theirs.**
A member cancels while the 18:00 reminder job is reading bookings. Task 1 subtracts one from every later `turn`, so two rows briefly share a number. Both members get a reminder for the same firing, and one of them turns up to load a kiln they don't hold.
Fix: each booking would sit at its own kiln and day, and a cancellation would change only its own row. This rewrites Task 1's data model and the gap-closing step that Task 3's cancellation depends on.
*Raised by: buildability, failure cases*

**2. Should the reminder job report that it ran? I recommend yes. Without it, reminders can stop and the first person to notice is a member who missed a firing.**
The 18:00 job fails one evening. Nobody is told, no reminders go out, and members miss their 8 am load. Nobody learns of it until a firing goes wrong. At production maturity, a failure nobody can see is a decision for you. The reviewer filed it as "your call".
Fix: `"email the technician when the job finishes, and again if it has not finished by 19:00"`, added to Task 2.
*Raised by: failure cases*

### Would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call

**3. Should the cancellation link cancel just by being opened? I recommend no: open a confirm page, then confirm the result. Otherwise members can lose firings they never meant to cancel.**
A member's mail system scans links, or the member forwards the email. Opening the link cancels the booking, the day goes to the standby list, and the member gets no message saying the booking is gone, so they can't tell whether it still stands.
Fix: the link would open a page asking the member to confirm, and after confirming they would see and receive a one-line confirmation. This changes Task 3.
*Raised by: security, ease of use*
