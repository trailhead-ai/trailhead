**What the reviewers found.** The plan gets the shape of the slice right, but it is careless about the order of the queue, which is the thing the spec exists to protect. Two reviewers independently found that Task 1 stores each person's queue position as a number and renumbers everyone behind them when someone leaves. If someone leaves while the 02:00 job is reading the queue, two people can be offered the same plot. That breaks the requirement that the person at the front of that site's queue is sent the offer (AC2), and the one that the people behind a leaver each move up one place (AC5). Around that, the reviewers found three other gaps:
- **Loading the paper list:** the 340-row import in Task 4 can't be previewed, undone or safely re-run.
- **Failures nobody sees:** if the nightly job doesn't run, offers just stop and nobody is told.
- **Things people trip over:** the accept link, the offer email's wording and the leave flow each give someone a way to lose a plot or their place without knowing it.

**How much holds up.** The renumbering finding is solid: it follows directly from what Task 1 says. The import finding is also real. Loading the society's only copy of a 340-person list is a migration, and at production maturity a migration with no rollback path is rated Critical. I raised the Reliability reviewer's "nothing signals that the 02:00 job did not run" from Important to Critical. Production failure visibility is one of the concerns whose severity follows the spec's maturity, and at production it is Critical. The reviewer had probably downgraded it only because each reviewer may raise at most two Criticals. I lowered the Advocate reviewer's Critical ("people may find the queue confusing") to Important. It names no concrete failure, and its fix (a help page) doesn't point at any particular defect. The Advocate reviewer's two Important findings are the ones about real user harm. The Security reviewer was cautious given that no authentication design exists yet, but the accept-on-open link is a real problem. Link scanners in mail clients follow links automatically, so plots could be accepted without anyone choosing to.

**What the reviewers suggest.** Replace the stored place number with the join time and work out each person's place when it's needed. That removes the double-offer race and makes leaving the queue, and going to the back after a lapse, simple. Run the import under a batch id, with a dry-run preview and a way to remove a whole batch. Have the nightly job report that it ran, and raise an alarm when it hasn't. Then tidy up the Important findings: a grant should close the person's queue row, the accept link should go through a confirmation page, the offer email should state the 14-day rule up front, leaving should be confirmed, and the spreadsheet should be deleted once the import is checked. How to deal with each Critical is your call, not mine.

### Critical

**One freed plot can be offered to two people.**
Task 1 stores a `place` number and, when someone leaves, subtracts one from everyone behind them. If that happens while the 02:00 job is reading the queue, two rows can both hold place 1, and both people get an offer email for the same plot.
Fix: store the join time, order the queue by it, and work out each person's place when it's displayed. Never store a place number.
*(Builder, Reliability: 2 reviewers)*

**Importing the paper list can't be checked first or undone.**
Task 4 loads about 340 rows in one run from the warden's laptop, with no preview and no rollback. Run it twice and everyone is on the list twice. If it stops halfway, nothing records where it stopped. This is the society's only list, and at production maturity a migration without a rollback path is Critical.
Fix: tag every imported row with a batch id, show a preview of what will be loaded, and let a whole batch be removed.
*(Reliability)*

**If the nightly offer job stops running, nobody finds out.**
Nothing reports whether the 02:00 job ran, so offers silently stop until people write in to ask. At production maturity, failures nobody can see are Critical. The reviewer rated it Important, probably only because of the two-Critical limit.
Fix: email the warden when the job finishes, and send an alert if it hasn't finished by 03:00.
*(Reliability; production failure visibility, raised to Critical by this spec's production maturity)*

### Important

- **Someone given a plot out of turn can still be offered another one.** Task 3 records the grant but leaves the person's queue row open, so the nightly job will offer them a second plot. Fix: make the grant close that person's queue row. *(Builder)*
- **A forwarded email or a mail scanner can accept a plot for someone.** The offer link accepts the offer as soon as it's opened, and nothing ties it to the person it was sent to. Fix: make the link open a page that asks the person to confirm. *(Security)*
- **People can lose their place without being warned.** The offer email doesn't say that ignoring it for 14 days sends them to the back of the queue. Fix: say so in the first paragraph. *(Advocate)*
- **Someone who leaves by mistake can't tell whether they're still on the list.** Leaving gives no confirmation. Fix: show and email a one-line confirmation. *(Advocate)*
- **The spreadsheet of 340 names and email addresses stays on the warden's laptop.** Fix: delete it once the import is verified, and say so in Task 4. *(Security)*
- **People may find the online queue confusing.** Lowered from Critical because it names no concrete failure. Fix: decide whether this needs anything beyond the items above. *(Advocate)*

### Minor

- The history page calls an expired offer a "lapse", which reads as jargon. *(Advocate)*
