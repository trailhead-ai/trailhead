Here is the detail on the three findings that would build the wrong thing and need your decision. All three are from the spec review of the kiln booking sheet.

---

**Anyone holding a forwarded confirmation email could cancel a booking (C1)**: fixed in the spec

You need to decide whether a cancellation has to be confirmed by the member who made the booking. I recommend it should, because otherwise anyone who sees a member's confirmation email can take that member's firing away.

The spec says "A member can cancel a booking up to 24 hours before its day. The day is freed and offered to the first member on that kiln's standby list" (AC3). It never says how a cancellation is tied to the member who booked. Confirmation emails get forwarded to a partner, a classmate or the studio's shared inbox. Whoever holds one cancels the booking. The day goes straight to the first member on standby. The member who booked turns up with their work and finds their firing gone, with no record of who cancelled it.

Fix: this replaces the cancellation criterion (AC3) with:

> **AC3.** A member can cancel a booking up to 24 hours before its day. The cancellation takes effect only after the member confirms it from a link sent to the email address the booking was made with. Once it is confirmed, the day is freed and offered to the first member on that kiln's standby list.

*Raised by: security.*

---

**A booking takes the whole kiln for a day, but a firing is shared (C2)**: needs a rewrite (`record-only`)

You need to decide what a member actually books: the whole kiln for a day, or space on its shelves in one firing. I recommend shelf space, because booking whole days lets one bowl tie up a kiln while other members wait weeks.

The spec says "A kiln holds one firing per day. A second booking for the same kiln and day is refused and the next free day is offered instead" (AC2). At the studio, a firing is a load of several members' work, and a kiln is full when its shelves are, not when someone has taken its day. As written, the first member to book a day holds it however little they bring. With two kilns for the whole membership, the wait for a free day grows while the kilns go out part-empty.

Fix: members would book shelf space in a firing, and a firing would close when its shelves are full rather than when one person has claimed the day. This rewrites the Objectives and these criteria:
- booking a firing (AC1)
- one firing per day (AC2)
- cancelling and handing the day to standby (AC3), so a cancellation frees shelf space and not a whole day
- the evening-before reminder (AC5)

No other spec is affected. Caveat: the right-problem pass rated its own confidence medium, because the spec does not say how often a firing is actually shared. If you know that members normally fire alone, reply "answer C2:" followed by what you know, and this is re-assessed on that basis.

*Raised by: right problem.*

---

**Members are not told when they book that shelves must be loaded by 8 am (C3)**: fixed in the spec

You need to decide whether the confirmation email should state the 8 am loading time. I recommend it should, because a member who doesn't know the time loses their firing.

The spec says "A member books a firing by giving a name, an email address, the kiln (large or small) and a day, and receives a confirmation email stating the booking" (AC1). That email gives only the booking. The 8 am time first appears in "The evening before a firing, the member receives a reminder listing what must be on the shelves by 8 am" (AC5). A member who plans around the confirmation, or who never sees the reminder, arrives at 9 to a kiln that is already packed and loses the firing.

Fix: `"Your work must be on the shelves by 8 am on the day of the firing."` is added to the confirmation email in the booking criterion (AC1). Caveat: the evening-before reminder (AC5) already states the time, so this finding is narrower than the ease-of-use pass put it. The real gap is that the member learns the time only the night before, and not at all if the reminder goes astray. The edit is still worth making, because it costs one sentence.

*Raised by: ease of use.*
