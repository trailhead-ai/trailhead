Here are all three findings in full. The spec review rates each as "would build the wrong thing, needs your decision". I propose fixing all three in the spec, and the edit for each is already written below.

---

**A member isn't told what goes back in the case when the loan starts (C1)**
*Proposed outcome: fixed in the spec. Raised by: ease of use.*

Should the confirmation email list what goes back in the case? I recommend it. Right now a member first sees that list in the reminder, and on a short loan that reminder never arrives. The cost is a missing finder cap the member is asked to replace.

The spec lists the case contents in only one place: the reminder sent two days before the return date (AC5). The confirmation email only "states the loan and its return date" (AC1). On a loan of two nights or fewer, the reminder's send date falls on or before the day the loan starts. So a member who borrows on Friday and returns on Saturday is never told about the finder cap, and the cap doesn't come back. The ease-of-use reviewer said members are never told at all. That goes too far, since the reminder does tell them. The gap is real for short loans, and short loans are exactly what the second finding expects more of.

Fix: `"Everything goes back in the case: the telescope, its eyepieces and the finder cap."` added to the confirmation email in the rule that a member borrows by giving a name, email, telescope and start date and gets a confirmation (AC1). Every loan then carries the list from day one, and the two-day reminder (AC5) still sends it again on longer loans.

---

**A loan ties up a whole telescope for the full dates asked, even for one night (C2)**
*Proposed outcome: fixed in the spec. Raised by: right problem.*

Should a member borrow a telescope by the night instead of from a start date to a club-set return date? I recommend it, but you know this better than the reviewers do. The stake is the club's most wanted scope sitting unused in someone's car while other members wait weeks for it.

A member borrows by giving a start date (AC1), and the system refuses any second request for "the same telescope and dates" (AC2). Suppose a member wants the largest scope for one clear Saturday night. They hold it for the whole loan period. Everyone else's requests in that period are refused and pushed to the next free start date, even though the scope is used for one night of it. The reviewer gave this medium confidence because the spec doesn't say how often a loan is for a single night. If your members almost always borrow for a full weekend, or a fixed loan length is club policy, say so with "answer C2". I'll then write that bound into the spec instead.

Fix: a member would ask for the nights they want, and a one-night request would hold the scope for that night only. This rewrites the borrowing rule (AC1) and the one-member-at-a-time rule (AC2):

    AC1. A member borrows a telescope by giving a name, an email address, the telescope, a start
      date and the number of nights, and receives a confirmation email that states the loan and its
      return date.
    AC2. A telescope is on loan to one member on any night. A request that overlaps a night already
      on loan is refused and the next free run of the same number of nights is offered instead.

Because this makes short loans common, the first finding's fix becomes necessary rather than nice to have.

---

**A scope left in the cupboard shows as free with no condition note (C3)**
*Proposed outcome: fixed in the spec. Raised by: failure cases.*

Should a returned telescope show as free only after someone has written a condition note? I recommend it. The spec's own Problem is that "nobody can tell afterwards who had a scope when its mirror was scratched". As written, the spec rebuilds that exact gap.

The return rule (AC4) assumes the loan officer is there on the return date to check the telescope. It also marks the scope free on the calendar "from that day" either way. Here's how that goes wrong: a member leaves the scope in the cupboard on Sunday evening and nobody checks it. The calendar shows it as free, and the next member collects it on Monday and finds the mirror scratched. The history (AC6) lists both loans, and neither has a condition note, so nobody can say which loan did the damage.

Fix: the calendar would show a scope as "returned, not yet checked" until someone writes its condition note, and as free only after that. This rewrites the return rule (AC4):

    AC4. On the return date the loan officer records the telescope as back, with a note of its
      condition. The telescope shows on the club's loan calendar as returned and not yet checked
      until the condition note is recorded, and as free from then on.
