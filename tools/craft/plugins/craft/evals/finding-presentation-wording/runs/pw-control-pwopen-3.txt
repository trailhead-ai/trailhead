**Gauntlet: telescope loan desk spec, round 1.** maturity: production (basis: stamp). No concerns were waived.

**What the passes found.** The passes clustered around two places. The first is the hand-back. As written, the condition note on a returned telescope depends on the loan officer being there on the return date, and a confirmation or reminder email that never arrives leaves no trace. That undercuts two things the spec promises: that the club can tell who had a scope when its mirror was scratched, and that the loan reaches the right member. The second is the edges of the criteria. The objective that a member can see where their loan stands is never delivered past the first email. The waiting-list offer is neither recorded nor closed. The 48-hour cancel window has no fixed starting point. Nothing says who can read the condition notes or who can cancel a loan.

**Whether it holds, and where it came from.** The failure-cases pass found the strongest point. Read plainly, the condition note in "records the telescope as back, with a note of its condition, and the telescope shows as free" (AC4) is skipped whenever a member drops a scope in the cupboard. That is exactly the scratched-mirror problem the spec was written to fix. Its point about bounced emails was filed as worth fixing. But at production maturity, a failure nobody sees is a decision for you, so I have raised it. The right-problem pass's proposal to lend by the night depends on how often members want a single night, and its own confidence line says the spec doesn't know. So I've moved it down to worth fixing. Its other claim, that the spec builds more than the complaint needs, misreads the Problem, which lists three complaints, not one. The ease-of-use pass's missing-finder-cap scenario overlooks the reminder that lists the case contents two days before return (AC5), so I've moved it down too. Where passes agreed independently, I trusted the finding more: self-consistency with ease of use on the loan-status gap, buildability with self-consistency on the waiting list, left-undecided with failure cases on the 48-hour clock, and security with left-undecided on who sees the history. The fact check found that the club has seven telescopes, not six. Nothing in the spec depends on that number.

**What to do about it.** Close the hand-back in two edits: a telescope stays "returned and unchecked" until its condition note is recorded, and a failed email is shown to the loan officer.
