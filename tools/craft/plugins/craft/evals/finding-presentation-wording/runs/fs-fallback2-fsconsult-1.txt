## What the panel found

The panel agrees that letting members start the large kiln with a code from the booking sheet breaks the committee's rule that nobody's work should be lost to someone else's mistake, and it breaks it in two ways. First, nothing stops a member from overloading the shelves, and one cracked shelf ruins everyone's pots in that load. Second, when that happens, the studio has no record of who loaded or started the firing, so the members who lost work have nobody to ask. A shared code makes the second problem worse, because anyone who learns it, including a stranger, can start the kiln. The smaller points are about members knowing the rules before they arrive: that the large kiln needs the induction stamp, what the evening slots are called, and how fair the arrangement feels.

## Whether it holds

The buildability and failure-case reviewers came at the question separately and both ended up at the same answer: the technician starts every firing. That answer holds, because it is the only one that deals with the overloading risk. Keeping a record would tell you who cracked the shelf, but it would not save the pots. The security reviewer's per-member code is a good idea, but its real value is that it makes a start traceable to one person, so it belongs with the record-keeping concern rather than standing on its own. The ease-of-use reviewer's main worry, that members "might feel" the rules are unfair to people who can't come in the evenings, is a guess about how people will react. It names nothing that actually goes wrong, so I've moved it out of the needs-your-decision group. The induction-stamp point from the same reviewer is concrete and cheap to fix. One thing no reviewer pressed: if the technician starts everything, the committee needs to know the technician can handle the demand. The failure-case reviewer's minor note about counting a full quarter of firings matters more than its rating suggests.

## What to do

On the committee's own rule, keep the technician as the only person who starts the large kiln. Members can still load their own work: they leave their loaded kiln furniture in a queue by 5 pm, and the technician checks the load and starts the firing. The queue should record who loaded each shelf, so a bad firing can still be traced. Before you commit, count a full quarter of firings to check that the technician can keep up with evening and weekend demand. If member-run firings come back on the table later, the minimum would be a code for each member, every start logged, and a check on the shelf limit. Meanwhile, add the induction-stamp line under the booking button now.
