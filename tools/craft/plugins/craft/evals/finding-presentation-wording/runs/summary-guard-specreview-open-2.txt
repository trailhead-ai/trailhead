The passes kept coming back to one weakness: every step in this spec depends on an email reaching the right person. Two passes looked at an offer that is never delivered, from different sides. The failure-cases pass saw a freed plot sitting empty for 14 days. The ease-of-use pass saw a person who has waited for years dropped to the back of the queue without being told. The right-problem pass found the other half: joining requires an email address (AC1). The paper list exists because the warden phones people, so anyone without email can't join once the paper list is retired. Beyond those two, the remaining findings are gaps a builder would have to guess at. Nothing says who reports that a plot has come free, what happens when a site's queue is empty, or how a person checks their place after the first email.

The convergent finding holds up best. Two passes that couldn't see each other described the same event, and it follows from the text of AC2 and AC3 alone. I kept the no-email finding at "needs your decision" even though its pass couldn't say how many people lack email. The exclusion follows from AC1's own wording, whatever the number is, and how the existing list moves online is a decision this production-maturity spec has to make. The fact check found one wrong figure: the committee minutes of 3 March record 412 names, not about 340. That changes the scale of the problem, not the design. The divergence pass's "pins" are real choices, but each one is a sentence, not a reason to rethink the spec. I dropped notes that were only about wording (whether to say "lapse", whether to name the mail provider).

What I propose: tighten the offer criteria so an offer that can't be delivered doesn't cost anyone their place, and give people without email a phone route handled by the warden. Both fit inside the spec's own sections, so it can advance this round.
