**Council review: allotment waiting list, first build**

maturity: production (basis: stamp). No stand-downs, and no waivers were tried.

**What the reviewers found.** The reviewers found three problems. The biggest is how the plan stores queue order. Task 1 keeps a `place` number and renumbers every later row when someone leaves. The 02:00 offer job in Task 2 reads that same number. If the two run at the same moment, two people can hold place 1 and both be offered the same plot. That breaks the spec's promise that a freed plot goes to the person at the front of the queue (AC2). The second problem is the one-off import of the 340-person paper list in Task 4. It has no preview and no way to undo it, and it is the only source of the real queue. The third problem is that the nightly job, which is the only thing that sends offers, fails silently. Alongside those are a handful of smaller holes: in the offer flow, in what an out-of-turn grant leaves behind, and in what people are told.

**Which findings hold up.** Builder and Reliability found the renumbering race separately, and both traced it straight to Task 1's wording. It is real and it is the core defect. Reliability's import finding also holds: at production maturity, a migration with no rollback is Critical. I raised one finding above the level its reviewer gave it. Reliability rated the silent 02:00 job Important, but at production maturity the calibration rates "nobody can see that it failed" as Critical, and here the failure is concrete: offers stop and nobody notices. I lowered one finding. Advocate's Critical, that people may find the queue confusing, names no moment where a user gets stuck, so it is speculative and is now Important. Security's offer-link finding stays Important, but it is sharper than that rating suggests. Mail scanners routinely open links, so an offer could be accepted for someone who never saw it. I would fix it together with the Critical findings.

**What to do about it.** Drop the stored place number and order each site's queue by a timestamp. The timestamp is set on joining and reset when an offer lapses, which also gives "goes to the back of the queue" (AC3) a clean meaning. The import then has to write that timestamp in the paper list's order, so Task 4 changes with Task 1 anyway. While it is open, give the import a batch id and a dry run, so a bad run can be previewed first and deleted whole. Have the 02:00 job report that it finished, and raise an alarm when it hasn't. Then make the offer link open a confirm page rather than accept on open, and have an out-of-turn grant close the person's queue row.

### Critical

**1. One freed plot can be offered to two people at once.** *(Builder, Reliability)*
Task 1 stores each person's queue position as a number and, when someone leaves, subtracts one from every later number. The 02:00 job emails whoever has the lowest number. If someone leaves while the job is running, two rows briefly share place 1 and both people get an offer email for the same plot. That breaks the rule that a freed plot goes to the person at the front of that site's queue (AC2). It also breaks the rule that the people behind a leaver each move up one place (AC5).
Fix: store a queue timestamp (set on joining, reset when an offer lapses) and order by it. Never store a place number. Show a person's place by counting the rows ahead of them.

**2. The paper-list import can't be checked first or undone.** *(Reliability)*
Task 4 loads about 340 rows from the warden's laptop in a single run, with no preview and no way to undo it. Run it twice and everyone is on the list twice. If it stops halfway, nothing shows where it stopped. This is the only copy of the real queue going in. Migration with no rollback is rated Critical at production maturity.
Fix: write every row under a batch id, offer a dry run that shows what would be inserted, and allow a whole batch to be removed. If you take Critical 1's fix, the import must also set queue timestamps in the paper list's order.

**3. If the nightly offer job stops, nobody finds out.** *(Reliability; raised from Important)*
Offers only go out from the 02:00 job. If it doesn't run, freed plots sit unoffered and the first anyone hears of it is a member writing in. At production maturity, a failure nobody can see is Critical.
Fix: email the warden when the job finishes, and send an alert if it hasn't finished by 03:00.

### Important

**Opening the offer email's link accepts the plot, even if someone else opens it.** *(Security)*
The link accepts the offer the moment it is opened, and nothing ties it to the person it was sent to. A forwarded email, or a mail scanner that opens links automatically, accepts the plot without the person ever deciding.
Fix: make the link open a page where the person confirms.

**A person given a plot out of turn stays in the queue.** *(Builder)*
Task 3 records the grant but never closes the person's queue row, so the nightly job can later offer them a second plot.
Fix: have the grant close that person's row.

**The paper list stays on the warden's laptop after the import.** *(Security)*
The spreadsheet holds 340 names and email addresses and has no stated end of life.
Fix: delete it once the import is verified, and write that step into Task 4.

**People aren't warned that ignoring an offer sends them to the back of the queue.** *(Advocate)*
The offer email doesn't say what happens after 14 days, so people will lose their place without knowing it could happen.
Fix: say it in the first paragraph of the offer email.

**Leaving the queue gives no confirmation.** *(Advocate)*
Someone who clicked by mistake can't tell whether they are still on the list.
Fix: show and email a one-line confirmation.

**The queue may confuse people once it is online.** *(Advocate; lowered from Critical: it names no moment where a user gets stuck)*
Fix: if a concrete point of confusion turns up, address it there. A general help page is optional.

### Minor

- **A grant's reason may expose personal details.** The reason a warden types for an out-of-turn grant shows on the history page, which every committee member can read. *(Security)*
- **"Lapse" reads as jargon on the history page.** *(Advocate)*
- **No test covers an offer that is exactly 14 days old.** *(Reliability)*
- **Tasks 2 and 5 each format dates separately.** They could share one helper. *(Builder)*
