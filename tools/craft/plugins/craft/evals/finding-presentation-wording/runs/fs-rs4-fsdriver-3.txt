### Would build the wrong thing, needs your decision

**1. Drop the stored place-in-line number and order bookings by their day. If it stays, two members can be told the same firing is theirs.**
Task 1 gives every booking a `turn` number and subtracts one from every later booking when one is cancelled. If a cancellation lands while the 18:00 reminder job is reading, two bookings end up with the same number. Both members get a reminder for the same firing, and one of them loads a kiln they don't hold.
Fix: bookings would be listed in day order, so a cancellation changes no other member's booking. This rewrites Task 1 and touches the reminder lookup in Task 2.
*Raised by buildability and failure cases.*

**2. Make the reminder job report whether it ran. Without that, reminders can stop and no one notices until a firing is missed.**
Task 2 runs the reminder job at 18:00 and says nothing about what happens if it fails. Reminders stop quietly, and the first person to notice is a member who missed a firing. The failure cases reviewer rated this as worth fixing. Because this spec is at production maturity, a failure nobody would see needs your decision.
Fix: the technician would get an email when the job finishes and another if it hasn't finished by 19:00. This adds to Task 2.
*Raised by failure cases.*

### Would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call

**3. Make the cancel link ask the member to confirm, then confirm the cancellation back to them. Today a forwarded email or a mail scanner can cancel a firing, and a member who clicks by mistake can't tell whether their booking still stands.**
Task 3's link cancels the booking as soon as it is opened, and nothing ties it to the member it was sent to. Mail scanners that follow links would cancel firings on their own, and the member is never told either way.
Fix: opening the link would show a page asking the member to confirm, and confirming would show and email a one-line notice that the booking is cancelled. This changes Task 3.
*Raised by security and ease of use.*

**4. Remove a member from the standby list once they are offered a freed day. Otherwise the same member keeps getting offered days.**
Task 3 frees the day and offers it to the first member on standby, but never removes that member from the list. The next cancellation offers the same member a second day.
Fix: making the offer would close that member's standby entry. This adds a clause to Task 3.
*Raised by buildability.*

**5. Put the 8 am deadline in the reminder. Without it, members arrive after the kiln has been packed.**
The spec requires the reminder to list what must be on the shelves by 8 am (AC5). Task 2 sends only "the list of what goes on the shelves", so members show up at 9 to a packed kiln and lose their firing.
Fix: `"by 8 am"` after "the list of what goes on the shelves" in Task 2's reminder email.
*Raised by ease of use.*

**6. Delete the membership spreadsheet once the load is checked. As planned, it stays on the technician's laptop with 90 names and email addresses in it.**
The plan's notes load all 90 members from a spreadsheet before launch and say nothing about the file afterwards, so the members' contact details stay on a personal laptop.
Fix: `"Delete the spreadsheet once the load is verified."` added to the plan's note on loading the members.
*Raised by security.*

**7. Check whether the booking page needs extra help, not a separate help page. As raised, there's no concrete failure behind it.**
The ease-of-use reviewer said members may find the booking page confusing at first but named no step where a member gets stuck. I moved it down from "needs your decision" for that reason.
Fix: "add a help page", as the reviewer wrote it.
*Raised by ease of use.*

### Rough edges, nothing to decide

**9. Keep the technician's lock reason off the members' view of the calendar.** Every member can read the calendar page, and a reason typed for a repair lock may carry personal detail.
