The reviewers agree that two problems in the plan need your decision before anything is built. Under this ritual's rules that stops the run as a `plan-critical` escalation.

**What the reviews found.** The plan gives each booking a place in line, a `turn` number, and renumbers every later booking when one is cancelled (Task 1). Two reviewers saw the same race: a cancellation that arrives while the 18:00 reminder job is running leaves two members holding the same firing. That defeats both "a kiln holds one firing per day" (AC2) and the objective that a booking and its reminder reach the right member. The second problem is the reminder job itself (Task 2): nothing tells anyone when it fails to run. Smaller themes: cancelling has loose ends (no confirmation, the standby list isn't cleared, the link cancels just by being opened), the reminder leaves out the 8 am load time that "what must be on the shelves by 8 am" (AC5) requires, and there's a data-handling note about the membership spreadsheet.

**Whether it holds.** The buildability and failure-case reviews found the renumbering race independently, and the plan writes out the steps that cause it, so it stands. The failure-case reviewer rated the silent reminder job "worth fixing, your call". I moved it to "needs your decision" because this spec is at production maturity, and at production a failure nobody would see is a decision, not a preference. The ease-of-use review's top-rated concern, that members may find the booking page confusing, names no concrete way it fails, so I moved it from "needs your decision" to "worth fixing, your call". Its point about the missing 8 am time is the more solid one. All the security points are kept as raised: the cancel link, the spreadsheet, and the lock reason shown to every member.

**What to do about it.** Taken together, the reviewers suggest ordering bookings by their booked day instead of a stored turn number, and having the reminder job report to the technician that it ran. With those settled, the rest are small fixes inside Tasks 2, 3 and 5. Both decisions are yours; I've written none.

### Would build the wrong thing, needs your decision

1. **Should bookings keep a renumbered place in line?** The reviewers recommend no. If they do, two members can be told the same firing is theirs. Worst case: a cancellation lands while the 18:00 job is reading, two rows end up with the same `turn`, both members get the reminder, and one of them loads a kiln they don't hold.
   Fix: bookings would be ordered by their booked day, with no stored turn number and no renumbering on cancel. This rewrites Task 1 and touches Task 3's cancellation step. *(buildability, failure cases)*
2. **Should the reminder job report whether it ran?** The reviewers recommend yes. Otherwise reminders can stop without anyone knowing. Worst case: the job fails one evening, no member gets a reminder, and the first anyone hears of it is a missed firing. At production maturity this needs your decision.
   Fix: the technician would get an email when the job finishes, and another if it hasn't finished by 19:00. This touches Task 2. *(failure cases)*

### Would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call

- **The reminder omits the 8 am load time.** AC5 asks for it. Worst case: members arrive at 9 to a kiln that's already packed and lose their firing.
  Fix: "add a sentence to the reminder email giving the 8 am load time" (in Task 2). *(ease of use)*
- **Opening the cancel link cancels the booking.** Worst case: a forwarded email, or a mail scanner that follows links, cancels a member's firing without them knowing.
  Fix: the link would open a page that asks the member to confirm (Task 3). *(security)*
- **Cancelling gives no confirmation.** Worst case: a member who clicked by mistake can't tell whether their booking still stands.
  Fix: members would see, and be emailed, a one-line confirmation (Task 3). *(ease of use)*
- **A standby offer doesn't take the member off the standby list.** Worst case: the next cancellation offers the same member a second day.
  Fix: making the offer would close that member's standby row (Task 3). *(buildability)*
- **The membership spreadsheet stays on the technician's laptop.** It holds 90 names and email addresses, and the plan never says when it goes. Worst case: a lost or shared laptop exposes every member's contact details.
  Fix: the spreadsheet would be deleted once the load is verified, and the plan's Notes would say so. *(security)*
- **The booking page may confuse members at first.** I moved this down from "needs your decision" because it names no concrete way it fails.
  Fix: a help page. *(ease of use)*

### Rough edges, nothing to decide

- The lock reason a technician types shows on the calendar every member can read, and it may carry personal detail. Recommend keeping it off the member view. *(security)*
- No test covers a cancellation made exactly 24 hours before the booked day. Recommend adding one. *(failure cases)*
- Tasks 2 and 5 each format dates for display. Recommend one shared helper. *(buildability)*
- The calendar calls a freed day a "release", which reads as jargon. Recommend a plainer word. *(ease of use)*
