**Council review of the plan for the kiln booking sheet, first build.** All four reviewers came back: buildability, failure cases, security and ease of use. The spec's own stamp sets its maturity to production (`maturity: production (basis: stamp)`). No concern was waived.

**What the reviewers found.** Most of the findings land on the booking and cancelling flow in Tasks 1 and 3. Task 1 keeps a `turn` number for each booking and renumbers every later booking when one is cancelled. That one step can tell two members the same firing is theirs. Task 3 has two more problems around cancelling: the link in the email cancels a booking the moment it is opened, and the member who was offered the freed day stays on the standby list. The second theme is the 18:00 reminder job. If it doesn't run, nobody finds out until someone misses a firing. Also, its email leaves out the 8 am shelf-loading time. The rest are smaller points about how the calendar reads and how the member spreadsheet is handled.

**How far the findings hold up.** The buildability and failure-case reviewers found the renumbering race separately, and it follows directly from steps the plan writes down, so I'm confident in it. The failure-case reviewer rated the silent reminder job only "worth fixing". At production maturity, a failure nobody can see counts as "needs your decision", so I raised it to match the spec's maturity rather than going with the reviewer's own rating. The ease-of-use reviewer said members "may find the booking page confusing" and called it "needs your decision". It names no actual failure, so I moved it from "would build the wrong thing, needs your decision" down to "would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call". That reviewer's point that the calendar calls a freed day a "release" doesn't appear anywhere in the plan, so it says more about the reviewer's assumptions than about the plan.

Reading the plan against the spec, I also found two gaps that no reviewer named, and they matter more than they look:
- The spec says a second booking for the same kiln and day is refused and the next free day is offered (AC2). Task 1 instead takes the next turn number, which queues the booking rather than refusing it.
- The spec says a member can cancel up to 24 hours before the booked day (AC3). Task 3 has no such cutoff.

The missing 8 am time is a gap of the same kind: the spec says the reminder lists what must be on the shelves by 8 am (AC5).

**What to do about it.** Rework Task 1 so a booking is a kiln-and-day row: a second booking for a taken day is refused and the next free day is offered, with no turn number. That removes the race and covers the same-day refusal (AC2) in one change. In Task 3, make the cancellation link open a page that asks the member to confirm, add the 24-hour cutoff, remove the offered member from standby, and confirm the cancellation back to them. In Task 2, give the 8 am time and have the job report to the technician when it finishes, and by 19:00 if it hasn't.

### Would build the wrong thing, needs your decision

**1. Do you want to drop the renumbered `turn` number and order bookings by their booked day? I recommend it, because otherwise two members can be told the same firing is theirs.**
Task 1 subtracts one from every later `turn` when a booking is cancelled. If a cancellation lands while the 18:00 reminder job is reading, two rows end up with the same `turn`. Both members get the reminder, and one of them turns up to load a kiln they don't hold.
Fix: bookings would be held as one row per kiln and day and ordered by that day, with no turn number kept. This rewrites Task 1 and changes how Tasks 2 and 3 read bookings.
*Raised by buildability and failure cases.*

**2. Should the reminder job tell someone whether it ran? I recommend it, because this spec is at production maturity and right now the job fails silently.**
If the 18:00 job doesn't run, no reminders go out and nothing says so. The first anyone hears of it is a member missing their firing. Without the reminder, members also aren't told that the shelves must be loaded by 8 am (AC5).
Fix: the technician would get an email when the job finishes, and another if it hasn't finished by 19:00. This adds to Task 2.
*Raised by failure cases. Rated "worth fixing" by the reviewer, and raised here because a failure nobody can see needs your decision at production maturity.*

### Would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call

**3. The cancellation link cancels a booking just by being opened. I recommend having it ask for confirmation first, so a forwarded email or a mail scanner can't cancel a member's firing.**
Nothing ties the link to the member it was sent to. A mail scanner that follows links, or anyone the email is forwarded to, frees the day, and the day goes to the next member on standby.
Fix: the link would open a page asking the member to confirm the cancellation. This changes Task 3.
*Raised by security.*

*Raised by buildability.*

*Raised by ease of use.*

*Raised by security.*
