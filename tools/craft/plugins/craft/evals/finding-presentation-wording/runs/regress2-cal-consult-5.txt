The two most serious findings come from the shared spreadsheet itself. Under that option, two things change the same sheet with nobody coordinating them: the sign-up form adds rows, and the Friday volunteer sorts and deletes them. The buildability review found that two members can take the last place in a lane at the same moment and both be told they're booked. The failure-cases review found that the Friday tidy-up can wipe out a booking without the member ever knowing. Both end the same way: a member turns up on Saturday for a lane they don't hold. The security review found a separate problem with the same cause. The sheet is shared by link, so every member's phone number can be read by anyone the link is passed to. The ease-of-use review was about members rather than storage: what happens to members who don't book on a phone, and whether the page is clear.

The double booking and the lost booking both hold. Each follows directly from how the spreadsheet option is described, with no guessing about how many people use it. I downgraded the ease-of-use review's lead finding, that members "might find an online page harder to use than a sheet". It went from "would build the wrong thing, needs your decision" to "would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call", because it guesses at how members will behave and names no concrete failure. The point behind it holds up better in its other form: members without a phone have no way to book. The migration and rollback findings are real but small, because this trial is at prototype maturity. Copying the data out in December, or going back to paper mid-term, is a chore, not a risk.

My recommendation is to store sign-ups in a small database behind the form from the start, and let the form be the only thing that writes to it. The form checks the lane's count before it confirms a booking, so double bookings can't happen. The volunteer gets a read-only list instead of an editable sheet, so the Friday tidy-up has nothing to delete. Phone numbers stay out of a link-shared file. That one choice removes both serious problems and the privacy leak. Alongside it: show members their booking on screen, add a cancel link, keep the paper sheet pinned up for the term, and write down the data layout for December.

### Would build the wrong thing, needs your decision

**1. Two members can both be confirmed for the last place in a lane. I recommend letting only the form write bookings, and having it check the lane's count first.**
Lanes hold eight. With the spreadsheet, the form and anyone editing the sheet are separate writers and nothing checks the count. If two members sign up for the last place at the same moment, both get a confirmation. One of them arrives on Saturday to a full lane.
Fix: the form saves one booking at a time and refuses to confirm once the lane's count reaches eight. A database behind the form gives you this from the start.
*Raised by: buildability*

**2. The Friday tidy-up can delete live bookings without anyone knowing. I recommend the volunteer never edits the copy the form writes to.**
If the volunteer sorts or deletes rows while members are still signing up, a booking can vanish. The member gets no notice and turns up for a lane they no longer hold.
Fix: the volunteer would work from a read-only list, or tidy a copy that replaces the live one only after sign-ups close. With a database behind the form, this is simply how it works.
*Raised by: failure cases*

### Would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call

**3. Every member's phone number can be read by anyone who has the sheet's link. I recommend keeping phone numbers out of anything shared by link.**
The page collects a name and phone number, and the spreadsheet is shared by link. Once that link is forwarded, every member's number goes with it.
Fix: phone numbers would be stored only where the volunteer can see them, either in a database or in a sheet shared with the volunteer alone.
*Raised by: security*

**4. Members can't easily tell whether a booking went through, so some will submit twice. I recommend showing the booking back to them.**
If saving is slow, the page gives no sign that it worked. Members resubmit and end up with duplicate rows. A vague confirmation also doesn't let them check that the booking is the one they meant.
Fix: after saving, members would see their booking on screen with the lane and time.
*Raised by: failure cases, security (2 reviewers)*

**5. Members who don't book on a phone can't book at all. I recommend keeping the paper sheet for the whole term.**
At the moment everyone books by writing on the sheet. Once the page replaces it, members without a phone or the habit of using one for this are shut out of Saturday sessions.
Fix: the paper sheet would stay pinned up beside the page for the term, with the volunteer copying its entries across. (Downgraded from "needs your decision": the related worry that members "might find the page harder" was a guess about behaviour, not a concrete failure. Explaining the page in the club newsletter, as suggested, is still worth doing.)
*Raised by: ease of use*

**6. Nothing says how a member cancels. I recommend a cancel link.**
A member who can't come has no way to give up their place. The lane stays full on paper while a place sits empty in the water.
Fix: each confirmation would carry a cancel link that frees the member's place.
*Raised by: buildability*

### Rough edges, nothing to decide

**7. Copying the data out in December will be done by hand.** This is migration and backfill: rough edges, nothing to decide, because this trial is at prototype maturity. Whatever replaces the trial will need the sign-ups copied across.
Fix: the trial's notes would record the data layout, so the copy is straightforward.
*Raised by: buildability*

**8. Going back to paper mid-term would mean re-entering that week's bookings.** This is rollback and reversibility: rough edges, nothing to decide, because this trial is at prototype maturity.
Fix: keeping the paper sheet pinned up, as in finding 5, would also cover this.
*Raised by: failure cases*

**9. The button label is technical.**
Fix: `"book my lane"` instead of `"submit"` on the sign-up button.
*Raised by: ease of use*
