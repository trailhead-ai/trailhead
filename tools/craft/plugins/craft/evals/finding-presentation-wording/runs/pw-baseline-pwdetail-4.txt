**The three findings that need your decision**

**C1: Members aren't told what goes back in the case until the loan is nearly over**

Should the confirmation email tell a member what belongs in the case? I recommend it should. Otherwise the only list a member ever gets arrives near the end of the loan, and a lost finder cap becomes the member's bill.

The worst case is a member who collects the scope and mislays the finder cap in the first week, because nothing said it was part of the kit. The spec has the confirmation email state only the loan and its return date (AC1). The list of the telescope, its eyepieces and the finder cap appears only in the reminder sent two days before the return date (AC5). By then the cap is already gone, and the member is asked to replace it.

Fix: `", and lists what goes back in the case: the telescope, its eyepieces, and the finder cap"` added to the end of the confirmation email in AC1. One caveat changes how much this matters. The reviewer said the member is never told, but I checked the spec, and that same two-day reminder (AC5) already carries the list. So the gap is when the member learns it, not whether they learn it. If your members get the scope with the cap already in the case, this is closer to "would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call".

*Raised by: ease of use*

**C2: A loan ties up a whole telescope for every date asked, even nights it isn't used**

Should a member book a telescope by the night instead of by a start date and a return date? I recommend booking by the night. The spec's own complaint is two members asking for the same scope on the same weekend, and date-range loans keep that clash in place.

The worst case is a member who books the club's largest scope for a weekend to catch one clear night. Friday is clear, and the scope sits unused on Saturday and Sunday. Meanwhile a second member asks for that weekend, is refused, and is offered only the next free start date (AC2), which may be weeks away. The spec ties every loan to a telescope and a start date (AC1) and a return date. So "one member at a time" (AC2) is measured in whole date ranges, never in nights.

Fix: members would pick the nights they want and add more as needed, and two members could share one weekend on the same scope. This rewrites the Objectives' "a loan and its return date". It also rewrites every criterion keyed to a start or return date: borrowing (AC1), the refusal of a second request (AC2), cancelling up to 48 hours before a loan starts (AC3), recording the return on the return date (AC4), and the reminder two days before return (AC5). The caveat: the reviewer rated this medium confidence, because the spec doesn't say how often a loan is a single night. You know that and the reviewers didn't. If most loans are a week of observing, date ranges fit and this finding loses its force.

*Raised by: right problem*

**C3: A scope handed back with nobody there to check it shows as free, with no condition note**

Should a telescope show as free on the calendar before anyone has checked its condition? I recommend it shouldn't. That gap is exactly how the spec's own problem, a scratched mirror nobody can trace, would happen again.

The worst case is a member who leaves the scope in the clubhouse cupboard on a day the loan officer isn't in. The spec says that on the return date the officer records the telescope as back with a condition note, and that the telescope shows as free on the loan calendar "from that day" (AC4). Free status is tied to the date, not to the check. So the calendar shows the scope free anyway, and the next member borrows it and finds the mirror scratched. There's no condition note in the loan history (AC6), so nobody can say whose loan did it. That breaks the Objectives' promise that every change to a telescope's record can be checked afterwards.

Fix: a telescope handed back with the loan officer absent would show as "returned, unchecked" on the loan calendar, and would show as free only once its condition note is recorded. This rewrites the return criterion (AC4) and adds that one state to the loan calendar.

*Raised by: failure cases*
