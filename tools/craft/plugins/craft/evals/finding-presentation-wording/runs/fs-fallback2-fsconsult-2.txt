**Maturity:** `maturity: production (basis: requested)`. The level was set by request, so nothing was read from a stamp. No concerns were waived.

## Synthesis

The four reviewers found three things wrong with the plan to let inducted members fire the large kiln with a code from the booking sheet. First, nothing in the code-and-form start stops a member from loading past the shelf limit. That breaks the committee's one stated rule, because a cracked shelf ruins every other member's work in the same load. Second, nobody records who loaded or started a firing, or that one was stopped early. When a firing goes wrong, the studio can't say whose pot burst, and the people who lost work have no one to ask. Third, one shared code from the booking sheet can be passed on, so someone with no induction, or a stranger, could start the kiln.

The buildability and failure-cases reviews are the strongest. They each reached the same conclusion on their own: the technician should start every firing. Their failure cases are concrete and follow directly from the design as described. Because the studio is reviewed at production maturity, a ruined firing nobody can trace counts as serious. The security point is real but smaller: it makes the record problem worse rather than causing work to be lost by itself. The ease-of-use reviewer's top concern, that evening rules might feel unfair, is a guess about how members would feel with no failure behind it, so I moved it down a level. That reviewer's practical points about the induction stamp and wording still hold.

My recommendation: keep the technician starting every firing of the large kiln. Members can load in the evenings into a queue the technician starts from, as the buildability review proposes. That meets the committee's rule without depending on every member checking the shelf limit. If the studio later wants members to fire the kiln themselves, three things should come first: a code for each member, a record of every start and early stop in the booking sheet's history, and some check against the shelf limit. Until then the door-code questions mostly go away.

## Findings

### Would build the wrong thing, needs your decision

**1. Overloading a shelf can ruin everyone else's work.** You need to decide whether members may start the large kiln themselves. I recommend not yet, because nothing stops the kind of mistake the committee has ruled out. Worst case: a member loads past the shelf limit, starts the firing with the booking-sheet code, a shelf cracks, and every other member's pieces in that load are lost.
Fix: members would no longer start the large kiln. The technician would start every firing, and members could leave loaded kiln furniture by 5 pm for the technician's queue. This replaces the evening and weekend self-start and the booking-sheet code from the proposal. The spec wasn't quoted in the reports, so I can't name which of its sections it touches.
*Raised by failure cases and buildability (2 reviewers)*

**2. A ruined firing can't be traced to whoever loaded or started it.** You need to decide whether every firing must carry a record of who started it. I recommend yes, whoever runs the firings. Worst case: a pot bursts and damages other work, the studio can't tell whose it was, and a dispute a month later can't be settled.
Fix: every firing would show who started it in the same calendar history the booking sheet already uses.
*Raised by buildability and failure cases (2 reviewers)*

### Would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call

**3. One shared door code can be passed on.** The code from the booking sheet works for anyone who knows it, so I recommend a code for each member if member access stays. Worst case: a member passes the code on, and someone with no induction, or a stranger, starts the kiln.
Fix: each member would get their own code. This would also show who started each firing (finding 2).
*Raised by security*

**4. An early stop leaves no record.** If members run firings, a firing stopped partway leaves no trace. Worst case: work comes out under-fired, and nobody knows the firing was cut short.
Fix: have the booking sheet ask for it when the member closes the door.
*Raised by buildability*

**5. New members only find out about the induction stamp when they're turned away.** I recommend saying so where they book. Worst case: a new member books the large kiln, comes in, and is refused at the door.
Fix: `"The large kiln needs the induction stamp."`, a sentence under the booking button.
*Raised by ease of use*

**6. Evening rules may feel unfair to members who can't come in the evenings.** I recommend explaining the rules openly. Worst case: members who can only come in during the day feel shut out of the new arrangement.
Fix: explain the rules on the studio's site.
*Raised by ease of use. Moved down from "would build the wrong thing, needs your decision" to "would cause trouble later, worth fixing, your call", because it guesses at how members would feel and names no specific failure.*

### Rough edges, nothing to decide

**7. Old codes keep working.** The code in the confirmation email should expire after the booked evening, so an old email can't open the studio. *Raised by security*

**8. The firing count is a rough figure.** Nine firings a month is an estimate. Count a full quarter before deciding. *Raised by failure cases*

**9. The wording could be shared.** The sheet could reuse the wording of the booking confirmation email. *Raised by buildability*

**10. The slot name sounds alarming.** Fix: `"member firings"` instead of `"unsupervised runs"` for the evening slots. *Raised by ease of use*
