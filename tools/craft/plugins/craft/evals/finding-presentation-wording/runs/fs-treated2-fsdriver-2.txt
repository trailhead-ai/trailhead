**Council review of the kiln booking plan**

maturity: production (basis: stamp). No waivers apply.

**What the reviewers found.** Most of the problems are in how bookings are numbered and how cancellation works. Task 1 gives each booking a `turn` number and renumbers every later booking when one is cancelled. That is the plan's most serious problem, and two reviewers found it on their own. It also replaces the spec's own rule, "A kiln holds one firing per day" (AC2), with a queue the spec never asked for. Task 3's cancellation has three gaps. The emailed link cancels as soon as anything opens it. The standby member who is offered the freed day stays on the standby list. The member gets nothing back saying the cancellation happened. The nightly reminder in Task 2 also has two gaps. Nobody would know if the job stopped running. The email leaves out the 8 am load time, although the spec requires a reminder "listing what must be on the shelves by 8 am" (AC5).

**How well the findings hold up.** The buildability and failure-case reviewers are right about the renumbering, and they agree on the fix. The failure-cases finding that a stopped reminder job goes unnoticed came in as worth fixing. I have rated it higher, as needing your decision. This spec is at production maturity, and at that level a production failure nobody can see calls for your decision. The ease-of-use reviewer's "the booking page may be confusing" names no concrete failure, so I have moved it down from needing your decision to worth fixing, your call. That reviewer's minor point about the word "release" refers to a word the plan does not use, so it overreaches. The security reviewer's cancellation-link finding is sound. Its other findings are reported in full below.

**What I recommend.** Rewrite Task 1 so the booked day itself is the booking slot: one booking per kiln per day, a second booking refused and the next free day offered, and no stored number to renumber. Make the cancellation link open a page where the member confirms, then confirm the cancellation back to them. Remove the standby member from the list once they are offered the day. Add the 8 am line to the reminder, and send the technician an alert when the reminder job does not run. Two findings need your decision, so the run stops here. I have not drafted a decision on either of them.
