The spec review (gauntlet) of the telescope loan desk spec: maturity is production (basis: stamp), no concerns were waived, and 2 of 4 facts were confirmed.

**What the passes found.** Most findings are about loose ends in the loan's lifecycle, not about the core idea of borrowing. The biggest gap is at the hand-back. "The loan officer records the telescope as back, with a note of its condition, and the telescope shows as free… from that day" (AC4) assumes the officer is there on the return date. If they aren't, the scope shows as free with no condition note, which is the scratched-mirror problem from the spec's own Problem statement. The second gap is email. The Objectives promise that "a loan and its return date reach the right member", but nothing tells anyone when a confirmation or reminder never arrives. Around those two sit smaller holes: the promised "see where the loan stands" has no criterion behind it, waiting-list offers are never recorded or closed, and the 48-hour cancel cut-off, who may cancel, and who may read condition notes are all left open.

**Whether it holds.** The failure-cases reviewer was right about the unchecked return. It rated the bounced-email problem only "worth fixing", but this spec is at production maturity, where a failure nobody can see needs your decision, so I raised it. The right-problem reviewer's call to lend by the night instead of by date range rests on a guess about how members use the scopes, and its own confidence line says the spec doesn't show that. The Problem talks about weekends, so I moved it down to "worth fixing, your call". The ease-of-use reviewer wanted the case contents in the confirmation email. That overreached, because "the member receives a reminder listing what goes back in the case" (AC5) already covers it, and what's left is the bounced-reminder case. The self-consistency and ease-of-use reviewers, working separately, both found the missing "see where the loan stands" criterion. That makes it the strongest of the "worth fixing" findings. I dropped three findings: naming the mail provider and the "next in line" wording are editorial, and whether the history is stored or rebuilt from events is a question for planning. The fact check found that the equipment register lists seven telescopes, not six. I haven't checked the register myself.

**What to do.** Fix both problems with edits to the spec itself: rewrite the return criterion so a scope counts as free only after a condition note, and add one criterion that shows the loan officer any email that couldn't be delivered. Nothing needs a rewrite of the spec's framing.
