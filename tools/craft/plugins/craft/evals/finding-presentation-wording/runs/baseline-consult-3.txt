**maturity: production (basis: requested)**. The maturity block reported no stand-downs and no unrecognised waivers.

## Synthesis

**What the panel found.** The panel kept coming back to one thing: a direct swap form would no longer have the warden acting as the one place that knows who holds which plot. Three problems follow from that.
- **Two swaps can collide.** Two tenants can agree overlapping swaps, and both go through.
- **Half-finished and past swaps aren't handled.** Nothing says what happens to a swap only one tenant confirmed, and no history is kept for settling a dispute later.
- **Swaps could bypass the waiting list.** A swap could hide a paid plot transfer. That breaks the committee's rule that nobody on the 340-person waiting list is passed over.

Separately, the Advocate questioned whether the form is worth building at all, given about six swaps a year and tenants who are mostly offline.

**Whether it holds.**
- **The collision is real.** The Builder and Reliability reports describe the same failure in different words. It follows directly from a design where two people confirm separately, so it stands as the one Critical.
- **The paid-swap risk is real but not proven.** The Security report is right that the committee's rule is at stake. How likely it is depends on whether plot sales actually happen, so Important is the right rating.
- **The Advocate's Critical was downgraded.** "Tenants might feel the process is unfair" names no concrete failure and guesses at how people feel, so it is now Important. Its real point is the Security concern seen from the user's side.
- **The Advocate's Important finding matters most.** It is the most useful point for the actual decision: six swaps a year by hand already works. Also note that the waiting-list spec's Non-Goals deliberately set swaps aside, so this decision falls outside the spec as written.

**What to do.** Keep the warden route as the only way to swap for now. At six a year, a form adds risk without removing much work. If the society does build the form later, add it next to the warden route, not in place of it. It would need all of these before launch:
1. One shared table of who holds which plot.
2. A check, at the moment of the second confirmation, that both tenants still hold the plots named.
3. Half-confirmed swaps that expire after a documented number of days.
4. Every swap written to the waiting list's history.
5. Warden approval for any swap that moves a tenant to a different site.

Because swaps are a Non-Goal in the current spec, any of this belongs in a spec revision, not in a change built on the side.

## Consolidated findings

### Critical

**A tenant can arrive to find a stranger on the plot they swapped for.** *(Builder, Reliability; 2 lenses)*
Under the form design, each tenant confirms against what they saw when they started. Suppose two swaps naming the same plot are confirmed minutes apart. Both get applied, because nothing checks who holds the plot at that moment. One tenant loses the plot they agreed to take.
Fix: keep one table of plot holders, and apply a swap only if both tenants still hold the named plots when the second one confirms.

### Important

**A swap could hide a paid plot transfer that passes over the waiting list.** *(Security)*
With no warden involved, a tenant could pay another for a good plot and file it as a swap. This goes directly against the committee's rule that nobody on the waiting list is passed over.
Fix: require the warden's approval for any swap that moves a tenant to a different site. Whether this matters depends on whether plot sales are a real concern for the society.

**Replacing the warden with a form makes swapping harder for tenants who are mostly offline.** *(Advocate)*
Six swaps a year by hand already works. A form-only route adds a step for the tenants least likely to use it.
Fix: keep the warden route, and add any form as a second way to swap, not a replacement.

**A swap only one tenant confirmed is left hanging.** *(Builder, Reliability; 2 lenses)*
Nothing says what happens if the second tenant never confirms. The pending swap could sit there and block or confuse later swaps of the same plots.
Fix: expire half-confirmed swaps after a fixed period. The Builder suggests seven days; the Reliability report asks for the reason behind whatever number is picked to be written down.

**A swap dispute a year later can't be settled.** *(Reliability)*
No record of past swaps is kept, so nobody can show who agreed to what. This counts as production failure visibility, which this production-level review rates Important here.
Fix: write every swap to the same history the waiting list uses.

**Tenants may see the swap process as unfair to people on the waiting list.** *(Advocate; downgraded from Critical)*
This was downgraded because it names no concrete failure and depends on guessing how tenants will feel. The real fairness risk is the paid-transfer finding above.
Fix: explain on the society's site how swaps work and how the waiting list is protected.

### Minor

- **Confirmation emails should name both plots,** so a tenant can tell the request is real. *(Security)*
- **Call the form "swap my plot", not "reallocate".** *(Advocate)*
- **The form can reuse the waiting list's email templates.** *(Builder)*
