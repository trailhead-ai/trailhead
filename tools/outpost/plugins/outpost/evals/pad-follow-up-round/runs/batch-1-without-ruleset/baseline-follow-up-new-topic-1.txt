**Build the text badge.** If you want the colour too, put a small coloured dot inside the badge.

- **Status needs to be readable without colour.** The dot and the strip only use colour. That fails WCAG 1.4.1 and doesn't work for colour-blind users. Invoices usually have five or more statuses (draft, sent, viewed, paid, partially paid, overdue, void), and nobody remembers what five colours mean. A badge that says "Overdue" needs no legend.
- **It holds up as statuses get added.** A new status like "Disputed" is just another label. With a dot or strip you'd need a sixth colour that can't be mistaken for the other five.
- **The left-edge strip would clash with row states.** Lists often use the left edge to show which row is selected, focused or hovered. Using it for status as well makes both harder to read.

What I'd change about your option B: don't push the badge to the very end of the row, where it lands in a different spot on every row. Give it its own fixed-width **Status column**, ideally next to the amount, so your eye can run straight down it. A fixed column also makes sorting and filtering by status simple, which people do a lot with invoices ("show me everything overdue").

If you want a coloured signal you can spot from across the room, put the dot inside the badge (`● Overdue`) or tint the badge background. You get the quick colour scan and the text stays the source of truth.
