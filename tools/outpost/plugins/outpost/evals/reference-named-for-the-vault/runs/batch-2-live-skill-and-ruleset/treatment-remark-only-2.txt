How dense the invoices get should decide it:

- **A, table rows:** better when an invoice has many lines (roughly 10 or more) and people compare columns like qty, unit price, tax and total across rows. It scans fast, aligns numbers and prints well. Inline editing is harder on narrow screens.
- **B, cards:** better when an invoice has a few lines that each carry more detail, like a description, attachments, notes or per-line status. It works well on mobile and gives space for actions on each line. Comparing values across lines is slower, and long invoices get tall quickly.

One question to settle it: **how many line items does a typical invoice have, and do people mostly read and compare them, or edit them one at a time?** If it's many lines and mostly reading, take A. If it's few lines, detail-heavy or mostly mobile, take B. If it's both, there's a third option: table rows that expand into a card-style detail panel.
