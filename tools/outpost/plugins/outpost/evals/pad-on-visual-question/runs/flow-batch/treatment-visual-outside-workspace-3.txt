**I'd build the left rail.**

- **It suits how admins use an audit log.** They narrow down step by step: pick a date range, then an actor, then an action, then a resource. A rail keeps every filter visible and one click away. The other two layouts make you open a menu or drawer for each change.
- **It fits wide monitors.** About 240px for the rail still leaves plenty of room for time, actor, action, resource, IP and details. On a wide screen, saving that space is the slide-over's only real advantage, and it doesn't matter there.
- **It has room to grow.** Audit-log filters tend to multiply: IP, outcome, source, tenant. A rail takes them easily. A top bar runs out of room at four or five controls and ends up with a "More" menu.
- **The cost:** you need a fallback for narrow screens. Collapsing the rail into the slide-over below a breakpoint covers this. Also add a row of active-filter chips above the table so the current query is easy to read and to clear one filter at a time.

**Top bar** is the right choice if you expect only three or four filters for good, since it's the cheapest to build. **Slide-over** fits users on small screens who filter rarely, which isn't your admins: it covers the rows they're filtering and hides which filters are on.

**So for standup:** do you expect this to stay at a handful of filters, or keep growing past four or five? If it'll grow, go with the left rail. If it really will stay small, the top bar is fine.
