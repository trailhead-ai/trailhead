**I'd go with 3 retries.**

**Wait time at the verify step.** With a few seconds between attempts, the worst-case wait before you tell the user something went wrong is roughly:

| Retries | Total attempts | Worst-case wait (≈3s gap, plus each attempt's own time) |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 4 | ~10–15s |
| 5 | 6 | ~20–30s |
| 8 | 9 | ~35–60s+ |

If your failures are slow timeouts rather than quick errors, each attempt adds its own timeout on top of the gaps, so the real numbers are higher. About 10–15 seconds of "sending…" is roughly what people will put up with. At 30 seconds or more, many will refresh, go back or give up, and that leaves them more stuck than an early, clear failure message would. More retries mean a longer wait in the worst case, which works against your main goal.

**Load on the provider.** When the provider is slow, every signup in that window sends `retries + 1` requests: 4×, 6× or 9× normal traffic, all landing on a provider that's already struggling. Retries also tend to bunch together. At 8 you're adding a lot of load to their slowdown.

**Extra retries rarely help.** When a provider is slow, it's usually either a brief blip that clears within a retry or two, or a longer slowdown where a few more attempts a few seconds apart still fail. 3 retries covers the brief blips. Retries 4 through 8 mostly fall in the longer case, where they add wait and load without getting many emails through.

**What makes 3 work is the "giving up" message.** If it gives the user something to do, like "We couldn't send your email just now. [Resend]", the user decides when to try again. That beats having the system keep trying while they watch a spinner. If giving up is a dead end and they have to start signup over, I'd still pick 3 but improve that message.

I'd only go to 5 if your logs show sends that failed 3 times and then succeeded on attempt 5 or 6, and your verify screen keeps people informed while it waits. I wouldn't choose 8 under these requirements.

I haven't looked at any code, since your working directory doesn't hold the signup flow. This is based only on what you described.
