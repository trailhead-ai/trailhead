**I'd go with 3 retries.** Choose 5 only if your logs show recoveries that happen after the third retry.

**How long people wait.** The user sits at the verify step for every attempt, so total wait is roughly attempts × (provider timeout + delay). Assuming about 3 s between attempts and a slow provider eating a few seconds per call:

| Retries | Total attempts | Rough worst-case wait |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 4 | ~15–25 s |
| 5 | 6 | ~25–40 s |
| 8 | 9 | ~40–60+ s |

Past about 20–30 s on a signup screen, many people reload, click "resend" or leave. That gives you duplicate requests and still leaves them stuck. So 8 doesn't actually help the people you're worried about. It mostly makes the failure slower.

**Load on the provider.** When the provider is slow, it's slow for every signup at once. With a fixed delay and no backoff, each stuck signup sends up to N+1 requests in a tight cluster. At 8 retries that's up to 9× normal traffic, aimed at a provider that's already struggling. That's the classic way a slowdown turns into an outage. 3 retries caps it at 4×.

**Duplicate emails.** When a call is slow rather than failed, the provider often did send the email and you just timed out waiting. Every retry is another chance of the user getting 2, 3 or 4 verification emails, possibly with different codes. Fewer retries means less of that.

**Why retries stop paying off after the first few.** Provider slowness tends to be either a short blip that clears in seconds or a real incident that lasts minutes. Retries 1–3 catch the blips. Retries 4–8 almost never catch a minutes-long incident, because they all happen within the same minute.

**When 5 would be the better choice.** If you have logs of these failures, check which attempt finally succeeded. If a meaningful share land on attempts 4–6, use 5. If almost all recoveries happen on attempts 2–3, which is the usual pattern, 3 is right.

When the retries are used up, the "give up" message matters more than the count. Something like "We couldn't send your email right now. [Resend]" gets people unstuck better than a few extra automatic attempts.

So: are you OK going with 3, or do you have failure logs that point to 5?
