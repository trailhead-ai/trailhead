**Which one do you want to build: inline (option 1) or queued batch (option 2)?**

I'd go with **option 1, inline**:

- **Speed for customers.** Most refunds are probably small. With option 1 they're paid within seconds. With option 2 the policy check runs at night and the payout runs at the end of the day, so a request that comes in in the morning may not be paid until the *next* evening. That's one to two days for a refund the policy already allows.
- **Failures stay small.** If one inline payout fails, you retry that one refund. If the end-of-day run fails, every approved refund for the day is stuck, and the fix has to happen that same evening.
- **Finance gets a steady trickle.** In option 1, reviews arrive during the day as requests come in. In option 2 they all land at once after the nightly job, so finance faces a morning pile and approvals made late in the day might miss that day's payout.

Option 2 wins in a couple of places:

- **Reconciliation:** one payout run a day is simpler to reconcile.
- **Fees:** your payment provider may charge less per payout when you send them in batches.
- **Fraud review:** the delay gives you time to catch duplicate or fraudulent requests before money goes out.

If reconciliation or fees are what really matters, there's a middle ground. Decide inline as in option 1, but send approved payouts in small batches every 15 minutes or so instead of once a day.

Two gaps to settle whichever you pick:

- **Rejection emails.** Neither flow tells the customer when finance rejects a request.
- **Duplicate payments.** Option 1 needs an idempotency key on each payout so a retry can't pay the same refund twice.
