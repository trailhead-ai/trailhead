## My pick: the numbered step list down the left

The problem is people quitting halfway. At that point the user is deciding whether the rest is worth it, and the indicator should answer two things: how much is left, and what it involves.

| | How far along I am | What's left | Can I go back? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Progress bar on top** | Yes, clearly | No. "60%" of something unknown | No |
| **B. Numbered list on the left** | Yes (✓ 1, ✓ 2, **3**, 4, 5) | Yes: "Invoice branding" and "Send a test invoice" | Yes, the finished steps can be links |
| **C. "Step 3 of 5" text** | Roughly | No. Three more screens could be anything | No |

- **B names the remaining steps.** Seeing "4. Invoice branding, 5. Send a test invoice" tells people the rest is short and easy. A bare number or a bar doesn't. Ticked-off steps also show the work they've already done, which gives them a reason to finish.
- **A** shows how far along people are, but not what the remaining steps contain. It works best when steps are about the same size, and setup wizards rarely are: a bank-account step is much heavier than a branding step.
- **C** is the cheapest option, and probably close to what you have now. It gives people the least to go on when they're deciding whether to stay.

**What B costs:** it needs a side column. On mobile it has to collapse into a "Step 3 of 5 ▾" header that expands to show the list. Step names also need to be short and clear, so expect some copywriting work.

**A caveat:** an indicator only helps if the drop-off is about not knowing what's left. If support's "halfway" lands on one specific step, like a bank-account or tax step that asks for something people don't have on hand, the indicator won't fix it. Check your step analytics. If one step is the problem, fix that step: let people skip it and come back later, or say upfront what they'll need before they start.
