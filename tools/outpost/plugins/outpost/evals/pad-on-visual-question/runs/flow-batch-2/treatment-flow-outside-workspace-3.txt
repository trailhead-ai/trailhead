I'd build **option one**, handling each request as it arrives. I've drawn both flows side by side on a pad:

file://<run>/xdg-state/outpost/pads/refund-approval-flow/index.html

**Why option one:**
- **Small refunds are paid in seconds, not the next day.** Those are most of your volume, and fast payment is where customers notice the difference. Under the batch option even a $5 refund waits up to a day.
- **Finance can start sooner.** Large requests reach a person as soon as they arrive, instead of only after the nightly run. Under the batch option a refund that needs review can wait a night and then another day.
- **A failure affects one request, not all of them.** If one payout fails, only that refund retries. If the end-of-day run fails, every customer due that day waits.
- **It's simpler to build.** It's one decision point and one payout path, with no scheduler and no extra queue just for holding requests.

**What option one asks of you:**
- Payouts have to be idempotent, keyed on the refund ID, so a retry can't pay a customer twice.
- Finance needs a review queue or inbox for the requests over the limit.
- Reconciliation happens per transaction instead of once per run. If finance wants a daily summary, a report can provide it without delaying the payouts.

**When option two would win:** your payment provider charges per payout or limits how often you can call it, finance contractually works from a daily batch, or the policy check needs data that only exists at end of day (for example, settled transactions). If any of those apply, batching may be the right choice.

**So the question is:** do you want refunds handled per request in real time (my pick), or held for the nightly batch? Is there a provider fee, a finance process requirement, or a data dependency that pushes you toward batching?
