**Go with 3 retries.**

**Wait time for the user.** If there are about 3 seconds between attempts, the worst case before the user hears anything is roughly:

| Retries | Total attempts | Worst-case wait (3s gaps + call time) | Load during an outage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 4 | ~10–15s | 4× normal |
| 5 | 6 | ~20–25s | 6× normal |
| 8 | 9 | ~35–45s | 9× normal |

Most people leave or reload a spinner after 10–15 seconds. With 8 retries, some users give up before the retries do, so the extra attempts don't help them. They also fire again if the user reloads and starts over.

**Load on the provider.** Short slowdowns usually clear by the first or second retry. If a send still fails after three retries a few seconds apart, the provider is probably degraded rather than briefly slow. More retries then mostly add load on a provider that's already struggling, and they do it at the worst time: every signup in progress multiplies its requests at once.

**Not leaving people stuck.** What fixes this is what happens after the retries run out, not how many there are. If the failure message comes with a "Resend email" button (or "we'll keep trying, check back in a minute"), giving up after 3 costs the user very little. If giving up leaves them at a dead end, no retry count will fix that.

**Two things to check before you settle:**

1. **Which attempt succeeds, according to your logs.** If you log retries, look at where sends succeed. If nearly all successes come on attempt 1 or 2, 3 retries is plenty. If a large share only succeed on attempts 4–6, the provider's slow periods last longer than your gaps, and 5 is justified. 8 is hard to justify with a gap of a few seconds either way.
2. **Duplicate emails.** "Fails when the provider is slow" often means the request timed out on your side but the provider still sent the email. Each retry can then send another one, and users end up with several verification emails, possibly with different codes or links. If your provider supports an idempotency key, use one. Otherwise make sure older links stay valid, or users will click a stale one and get stuck in a different way. This risk grows with the retry count, which is another point for 3.

Choose 5 only if your logs show successes landing late. Avoid 8.
